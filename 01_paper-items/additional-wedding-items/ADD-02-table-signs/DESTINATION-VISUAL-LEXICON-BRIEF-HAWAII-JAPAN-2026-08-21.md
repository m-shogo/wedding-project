Do **not** copy official tourism logos, proprietary graphic systems, protected identity marks, exact official palettes, brand lockups, destination taglines, or licensed library imagery. External sources are used only to improve cultural accuracy, photography/imagery judgment and place-derived art direction.

All final destination name, table number, date and country-theme copy remain native editable Figma text.

## Professional reference findings

### Hawaii Tourism Authority / Hawaii Tourism Japan

Official 2026 guidance emphasizes accurate and respectful depiction of Hawaiʻi, including natural environment, local identity, people's relationship with the land, culture, community and responsible tourism. Its published image/video pillars include landscape, culture, local cuisine, adventure and softer/breathtaking visual moods.

Practical transfer for this wedding table sign:

- Hawaii should not be reduced to a palm/hibiscus/tiki/ukulele collage;
- generic “tropical paradise” stock imagery is insufficient;
- landscape/light/ocean/materiality may carry atmosphere without inventing fake cultural symbols;
- if people are absent, the design can still feel Hawaiian through environment, air, light, material and hospitality rather than stereotypes;
- any real official/library imagery has usage restrictions and is not automatically reusable for this personal artifact.

Reference entry points:

- `https://www.allhawaii.jp/business/guidelines/`
- 2026 Hawaii Brand Guidelines linked there;
- Maʻemaʻe Hawaii Style & Resource Toolkit linked there.

### JNTO responsible photography / Japan travel guidance

JNTO's responsible-photography guidance explicitly notes that Japan's visual character is often found in small, less-obvious details and stresses respect, privacy and cultural awareness instead of chasing only iconic/social-media clichés.

Practical transfer for this table sign:

- avoid defaulting immediately to Mount Fuji / torii / geisha / sakura icon stacking;
- allow material, light, printed-paper behavior, small urban/rail/detail cues or calm rhythm to carry Japan when appropriate;
- do not invent a specific city/temple/route because the current table theme is country-level and no precise destination is authoritative.

Reference entry point:

- `https://www.japan.travel/en/responsible-travel-guide/features/responsible-photography/`

## Anchor 1 — HAWAII visual lexicon

### One-sentence emotional brief

`海へ向かう朝の光と、風が抜ける開放感を、観光ポスターではなく結婚式の小さなdestination printとして感じる。`

### Prefer

- broad horizon / open-air composition;
- warm side-light or late-afternoon glow rather than synthetic neon tropical color;
- ocean-air movement expressed through crop/texture/light direction;
- tactile print grain / risograph-like or screenprint-like fixed art if it remains elegant at actual size;
- one generous environmental gesture rather than many tropical icons;
- warm hospitality and breathing space around the native destination name.

### Avoid

- hibiscus/palm/lei/tiki/ukulele clusters used as shorthand;
- fake Hawaiian words or decorative pseudo-language;
- fake airline/tourism branding;
- glossy stock “paradise” image treatment;
- generated people, fake beach weddings or fake documentary moments;
- official HTA/HVCB logo or exact brand-system imitation;
- hyper-detailed low-contrast texture that disappears in print.

### Fixed-art role A — `COASTAL LIGHT FIELD`

Role:
- non-person atmospheric fixed art, not factual photography;
- approximately upper/right `35–45%` of a 1000×1480 sign;
- must leave a stable native-text lane through left/middle.

Candidate families if image generation becomes available:

1. soft coastal horizon / ocean light, editorial grain;
2. closer abstract water-light reflection, not identifiable location;
3. warm volcanic/mineral + ocean-air color field, without pretending to be a map;
4. paper/print texture derived from coastal light rather than literal scenery.

Reject:
- fake lettering/signage;
- warped coast/architecture;
- plastic water;
- HDR/synthetic travel-ad look;
- generic palm silhouette sunset.

### Native typography strategy

- HAWAII may remain a strong Latin masthead, but native Japanese theme headline should carry the emotional voice;
- do not rely on cropped `HAWAII` alone as the entire place identity—the previous D study proved that this becomes gallery-poster generic;
- large type may overlap the fixed-art safe zone only if contrast remains stable under replacement.

## Anchor 2 — JAPAN visual lexicon

### One-sentence emotional brief

`大きな観光記号ではなく、紙・光・細部・移動のリズムから、日本を旅するときの“気づく楽しさ”を感じる。`

### Prefer

- tactile paper / fibre / restrained print texture;
- precise alignment interrupted by one intentional asymmetry;
- detail-scale visual rhythm rather than icon collage;
- sunrise/vermilion as one accent only when compositionally justified, not as automatic flag symbolism;
- quiet-to-bold scale contrast that still feels celebratory;
- possibly a small transit/edge/threshold cue only if it remains non-factual and does not invent route data.

### Avoid

- Fuji + torii + sakura + kimono/geisha stacks;
- pseudo-Japanese seals or fake calligraphy;
- invented rail line, station, gate or ticket facts;
- tourism-board logo imitation;
- “minimal Japan” emptiness with no experiential warmth.

### Fixed-art role B — `PAPER + LIGHT DETAIL FIELD`

Role:
- non-person material/editorial fixed art;
- can occupy a side or lower field rather than the same upper/right role as Hawaii;
- must create a visibly different physical rhythm from Hawaii while remaining part of one table-sign family.

Candidate families if image generation becomes available:

1. macro paper/fibre + directional morning light;
2. abstract architectural shadow/detail with no identifiable protected/private place;
3. offset-print paper edge / layered sheet composition;
4. restrained urban detail texture that reads as material, not documentary location claim.

Reject:
- fake Japanese writing embedded in pixels;
- souvenir-shop cliché;
- anime/manga shorthand unless specifically required by an authoritative theme;
- photographic people or private-property implication;
- microtexture that fails actual-size print.

### Native typography strategy

- Japanese display type may become stronger than the Latin country label;
- keep native text fully editable and use the fixed-art role only as atmosphere/material support;
- do not reuse Hawaii's exact masthead proportion or image placement merely for family consistency.

## Family rule for the two-anchor experiment

Keep only these constants across both signs:

- physical size `1000×1480`;
- native semantic roles;
- consistent minimum safe area;
- one disciplined type family/weight logic where useful;
- date/table-number legibility tier;
- print-quality bar.

Allow these to differ by destination:

- dominant fixed-art role and crop;
- location of atmospheric field;
- color behavior;
- display-type scale;
- negative-space pattern;
- motion direction;
- surface/material language.

The family resemblance should come from editorial discipline, not a repeated circle, rounded stroke, fold, book, label or image box.

## Promotion gate for the next experiment

Do not scale to all 11 signs unless both anchor destinations pass:

1. destination name and table number readable at family thumbnail scale;
2. fixed art adds clear place-specific atmosphere without stereotype;
3. HAWAII and JAPAN are distinguishable even if their destination labels are temporarily hidden during internal review;
4. native variable copy remains structurally independent;
5. actual-size image/texture quality is credible for print;
6. no fake text/people/brand assets;
7. the pair still feels like one wedding table-sign family;
8. the result clearly beats current production on destination specificity **without** dropping below it on excitement/warmth.

If criterion 8 fails, keep current production and record the candidate as a learning experiment rather than forcing promotion.
