## Why reopen beyond the current FIELD JOURNAL

Fresh 500px / 1000px read-only review confirms the current front is disciplined, editable and physically plausible. Its remaining weakness is not structure. It is an art-direction ceiling:

- the first read is closer to a restrained modern notebook/book cover than to a once-in-a-lifetime departure object;
- travel is communicated mostly through words and the generic notion of a journal, not through a distinctive sense of movement, hospitality or destination discovery;
- the current large paper blocks are controlled, but the emotional amplitude between `旅の記録` and the wedding-day departure moment can be higher;
- simply adding airplanes, stamps, palm trees, badges or more bright shapes would regress into the exact travel-template vocabulary already rejected elsewhere in the suite.

Therefore the next experiment should **not** be another subtraction pass on FIELD JOURNAL. It should be a genuinely independent clean-room direction that tests whether stronger travel anticipation can coexist with the unmistakable role of a keepsake booklet.

## Professional research absorbed

### 1. Editorial design = theme + variation, not a repeated module system

Pentagram's editorial-design guidance describes strong publications as a balance of a familiar overall theme and variation that lets stories unfold in new and surprising ways. Transferable principle: a passport booklet should have one coherent voice but should create deliberate shifts in scale, density and pacing rather than a single repeated card/module grammar.

Source: https://www.pentagram.com/editorial-design

### 2. One authentic travel gesture is stronger than clusters of travel clichés

Pentagram's United Airlines work deliberately enlarges and pushes a genuine identity gesture out of frame to evoke the magic of flight. Transferable principle: travel excitement should come from one strong movement/gesture, not from many literal icons.

Source: https://www.pentagram.com/work/united-airlines

### 3. Travel brands need an emotional core and authenticity, not applied culture-polish

Lippincott's airline-brand analysis argues that successful airlines are clear about what they want to be known for, and notes Hawaiian Airlines as an example where culture and hospitality are operational foundations rather than decorative styling. Transferable principle: Hawaii / aloha should affect warmth, rhythm, generosity and hospitality of the paper experience, not become a pasted-on hibiscus/palm/tiki motif set.

Source: https://www.lippincott.com/ideas/looking-vertically/five-ways-airline-brands-can-fly-above-the-noise/

### 4. Curiosity / discovery can be a publication-level promise

Pentagram's DK work centers the publisher identity on curiosity and discovery while allowing the mark to adapt to each cover. Transferable principle: the passport can feel collectible and exploratory without becoming fake official travel documentation.

Source: https://www.pentagram.com/work/dk

### 5. Current Japanese graphic design should be judged across books/editorial/general graphics, not only wedding references

JAGDA's 2026 annual explicitly presents Book & Editorial Design, General Graphics, package and integrated work together as a current professional benchmark. Transferable principle: judge the passport as serious contemporary Japanese graphic design and print object, not only against wedding-stationery templates.

Source: https://www.jagda.or.jp/en/news/10423/

## Art-direction objective

Emotional target:

`旅立つ直前の高揚感 × ハワイに向かう光と風 × 結婚式当日に手渡される記念冊子`

The object must feel worth keeping after the wedding. It must not read as airline administration, fake immigration paperwork, a travel-app UI, or a generic luxury notebook.

## Three materially different clean-room directions

The next Figma authoring run should create all three from blank frames before comparing against any retained production.

### Direction A — `DEPARTURE WINDOW BOOKLET`

Core idea: the booklet itself behaves like a sequence of views opening toward departure.

- dominant gesture: one oversized curved/diagonal opening field crossing the cover, derived from the sensation of looking through a cabin/window/portal but **not** drawn as a literal airplane-window icon;
- composition: strong asymmetry, Japanese headline crossing or approaching the opening, date treated as a second focal anchor;
- emotional read: bright, anticipatory, breezy;
- avoid: window-shaped rounded UI card, airplane silhouette, runway numbers, boarding metadata;
- fixed-art candidate: composed paper/light/sky transition with subtle print texture and no text;
- native roles: title, names, date, place, variable copy;
- expected advantage: strongest immediate flight/travel energy;
- main risk: can become poster-like and lose booklet specificity.

### Direction B — `ISLAND FIELD GUIDE`

Core idea: a personal field guide to one extraordinary day rather than an official passport.

- dominant gesture: one large destination-derived contour / tide / wind drawing that behaves as a publication graphic, not a map or tourism logo;
- composition: Japanese editorial title + one generous discovery field + compact factual index;
- emotional read: curious, warm, destination-aware, collectible;
- Hawaii expression: coastal light, wind, water rhythm, generous hospitality; no tiki, lei cliché, hibiscus wallpaper or fake Hawaiian lettering;
- fixed-art candidate: high-quality abstract coastal contour / wind field, preferably generated/composed only if the role brief can guarantee no fake text and enough print detail;
- native roles: all authoritative copy and facts;
- expected advantage: strongest destination specificity without fake airline semantics;
- main risk: can become quiet/gallery-like if color and scale lack celebration energy.

### Direction C — `SUNLIT DEPARTURE FOLIO`

Core idea: a premium but playful travel folio assembled from a few large paper movements rather than many micro-ornaments.

- dominant gesture: one bold sweeping sheet/fold moving across the cover and intentionally leaving frame;
- composition: large Japanese display line, one cropped numeric/date gesture, one contrasting factual lane;
- emotional read: pop, joyful, celebratory, editorial;
- physical logic: use only folds/overlaps that visibly connect to a paper edge or insertion; no floating rails/tabs;
- fixed-art candidate: composed sunlit paper field / print grain; no baked final copy;
- native roles: title, names, date, place, copy;
- expected advantage: strongest wedding-day celebration energy while staying a print object;
- main risk: family-scale convergence with other bright paper/fold artifacts; compare against suite before promotion.

## Hybrid authoring plan

Do **not** force every decoration into native primitives.

- variable/factual/emotional wording: native Figma text;
- replaceable photography: none by default for the cover; add only if a clean-room direction proves that photography is the actual quality bottleneck;
- reusable silhouette/line art: editable SVG only if it has a genuine future edit/recolor role;
- fixed atmosphere / paper / contour / light field: composed/generated asset is preferred when it materially beats fragile micro-geometry;
- generated asset must contain no authoritative wording, route data, passport credentials, QR, names or date;
- if generation is used, require `role brief → 2–4 materially different candidates → critique → adopted candidate only → exact Drive master → Figma placement → three-scale QA`.

## Image-generation role brief if Direction B or C needs it

Role: `FIXED ART / DESTINATION ATMOSPHERE FIELD`

- target: portrait cover support, 1480×2100 working canvas, intended to occupy roughly 35–65% depending on direction;
- subject: abstract coastal light / trade-wind movement / water-surface rhythm / sunlit paper atmosphere;
- focal behavior: movement should lead toward native title/date, not compete with them;
- text-safe area: preserve at least one broad low-detail lane for Japanese native type;
- palette behavior: warm sunlight + ocean/lagoon family with one celebratory accent; avoid using every palette color equally;
- print character: editorial print grain / ink / paper tactility, no plastic 3D polish;
- negatives: no people, no aircraft, no passport, no stamp, no fake map labels, no fake text, no UI, no barcode, no QR, no resort-stock-photo cliché, no impossible coast/building geometry;
- candidate count: 3 materially different compositions minimum when generation is available;
- adoption test: must make the cover more memorable at 500px **and** retain clean native-title readability at actual size.

## Professional council questions

The receiving run should explicitly score:

- Executive Creative Director: Is there one memorable idea, or just styling?
- Graphic / Editorial Designer: Is the hierarchy authored through type, scale, rhythm and negative space rather than containers?
- Japanese Editorial Typographer: Are line breaks, punctuation, Japanese/Latin spacing and optical alignment intentional?
- Travel / Hospitality Art Director: Does the design communicate welcome, anticipation and destination warmth without tourism clichés?
- Print Production Director: Do claimed folds/edges/overlaps behave like credible paper at actual size?
- Image Editor: if an asset is generated, does it look authored and printable rather than stock/AI-polished?

## Stop conditions

Reject or method-switch if:

- a direction turns into a poster and no longer reads as a keepsake booklet;
- pop energy is achieved mainly by multiple unrelated stickers/badges/shapes;
- Hawaii is represented through cliché icon clusters rather than atmosphere/hospitality;
- generic English or fake travel credentials appear;
- composed fixed art fights native Japanese type;
- clean-room candidates do not clearly beat FIELD JOURNAL after maturation.
