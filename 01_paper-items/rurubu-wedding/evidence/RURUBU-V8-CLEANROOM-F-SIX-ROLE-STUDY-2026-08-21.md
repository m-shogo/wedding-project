## Professional research applied

Earlier V8 F research remains relevant:

- Pentagram book-design practice: book design orchestrates typography, layout, printing/binding and words/images as one storytelling experience.
- Massimo Vignelli / Pentagram: grid is structural infrastructure, not visible decoration; ingredients are organized page by page against a consistent grid.
- Pentagram `Build, Memory`: an open flexible grid can hold dense heterogeneous material while preserving breathing room; images can vary materially in size and depth.
- Pentagram Brian Lanker monograph: picture editing includes sequencing and pacing, not only selecting individually strong images.
- Pentagram Publications / Netflix Queue: publication coherence comes from theme + variation and deliberately varied pacing rather than repeating one feature template.

New/deeper research applied in the follow-up pass:

- Aperture, `How to Produce a Photobook`: editing, sequencing and designer involvement need to happen early; a physical dummy and page turning change the judgment of pacing, paper and binding.
- Aperture / Stuart Smith, `How Not to Design a Photobook`: the designer should contribute to sequence, picture editing, size/feel and reproduction quality rather than behave as a production-only operator; a tighter primary edit can coexist with a broader secondary edit.
- Aperture workshop material: apparently secondary or `b-roll` material can become valuable once the first sequence is assembled; coherence should be judged across the whole book rather than image by image.
- Aperture / Kummer & Herrman: audience/purpose, image sequencing, cover, typography, paper and binding are one design problem; collaboration should be willing to leave a comfortable first solution.
- JAGDA continues to treat Book & Editorial Design as a distinct professional design category, reinforcing that editorial structure, photography, editing and graphic design are related but separate responsibilities.

Local V8 hypothesis after this pass: a coherent publication does not require a large image field on every facing page. If one page carries a legitimate visual beat, an intentionally quiet text-led counter-spread can improve pacing and authenticity. Conversely, a blank colored box that only means `photo goes here` should not survive into a serious visual comparison.
