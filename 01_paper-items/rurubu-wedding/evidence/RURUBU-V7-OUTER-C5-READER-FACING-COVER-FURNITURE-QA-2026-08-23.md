## Fresh professional research used

New/deeper material for this run deliberately moved to Japanese magazine editorial voice rather than reusing the prior Hawaii-food references.

- POPEYE describes itself through a stable editorial identity (`Magazine for City Boys`) and its California issue documents editors living in LA and absorbing the culture before producing the issue. The useful principle is not to imitate POPEYE typography; it is that small cover language should express a defensible editorial identity or actual lived editorial content rather than generic magazine-sounding taxonomy.
- Tokyo TDC's current Japanese type-design work reinforces that Japanese display voice can be structurally expressive without depending on generic Latin labels.

Rurubu-specific hypothesis:

> On a high-energy Japanese travel-magazine cover, small English taxonomy that duplicates a visible Japanese service/index is not adding useful information. Removing the duplicate and letting the remaining microcopy speak in the publication's actual Japanese editorial voice should improve authenticity without reducing energy.
