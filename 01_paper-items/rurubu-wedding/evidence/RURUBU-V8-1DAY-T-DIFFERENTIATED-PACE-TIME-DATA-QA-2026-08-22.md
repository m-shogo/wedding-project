## Visible problem

J correctly bound the right-page timeline spacing to the actual schedule times, but the left page repeated the same four events as another time/action/descriptor list. The spread therefore used two neighboring pages to say nearly the same thing in two UI-like representations. At whole-spread scale this read as a wireframe/data-demo rather than a book spread with two different editorial jobs.

## Fresh professional research used

This run deliberately used a new source for this decision rather than recycling the immediately previous V8 references.

- Eye Magazine, `One week in pictures`: Richard Turley argues that editorial design can lose photography/content under excessive page-design paraphernalia; Mark Porter taught him the importance of imagery, pacing and scale. The transferable principle used here is not a literal layout: **a spread should spend scale and visual devices where they change editorial meaning, not repeat the same information because another module is available.**
- JAGDA `Graphic Design in Japan 2026`: Book & Editorial Design remains a distinct judged category in the 2026 annual, reinforcing that the spread is assessed as editorial/physical composition, not merely as correct information UI.

Research observations were treated as hypotheses, not promoted rules.

## Bounded experiment

A rollback-safe duplicate of J was created as T `2203:2`.

Right page:

- kept the existing time-scaled timeline and its exact four schedule times;
- no new facts, cards, images, gradients, shadows or decorative English were added.

Left page:

- hid the duplicated `10:00 / START`, `11:40 / CAFE`, `15:10 / WALK`, `18:30 / TABLE` rows;
- preserved the existing Japanese descriptors `海辺 / 長めに / 寄り道 / ゆっくり`;
- kept their vertical order/approximate time-position but turned them into an uneven typographic pace score;
- gave `海辺` and `寄り道` the strongest navy display weight;
- treated `長めに` and `ゆっくり` as quieter gray modifiers rather than arbitrary red accents.

The page-role split is now:

- **left = experiential pace / editorial mood**;
- **right = exact time/data reading**.

No V6/V7 image or generated asset was reused.

## Rejected intermediate treatment

The first T pass colored `長めに` and `ゆっくり` red. It created an attractive rhythm but the accent had no defensible semantic owner; it was styling-by-alternation. That pass was rejected. The two modifier words were changed to the existing intro-gray and regular weight.
