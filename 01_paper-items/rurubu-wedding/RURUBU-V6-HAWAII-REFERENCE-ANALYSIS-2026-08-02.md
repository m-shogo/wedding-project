## Sources reviewed

### R01 — るるぶハワイ'26

- Publisher page: https://books.jtbpublishing.co.jp/e-book/60001-202506145525-000/
- Production showcase: https://www.ark-gr.co.jp/works/rurubu-hawaii-26/
- Type: cover / current Hawaii Rurubu direction

Observed mechanisms:
- one dominant destination photograph carries most emotional weight
- saturated pink works as a framing field rather than many independent colored cards
- large white title field creates a stable silhouette over a visually active photograph
- issue year is isolated as a small high-contrast burst
- a vertical side promise creates one secondary reading axis
- supporting cover lines are grouped and subordinate rather than six equal boxes
- scenery, person, and title are arranged so the destination remains immediately recognizable at thumbnail size

Adopt:
- one unmistakable destination hero
- one main title silhouette
- one vertical or edge-aligned secondary hook
- maximum three supporting cover-line groups
- strong color used as a frame and navigation device

Reject:
- exact pink/white title silhouette
- exact title position
- exact vertical ribbon wording and placement
- copying the commercial cover hierarchy one-for-one

V6 application:
- create an original `るるぶWEDDING HAWAII` masthead relationship
- use a newly generated resort/ocean hero with text-safe sky or water
- use a warm-white/sand title field with coral or hibiscus-pink accent
- reserve sunshine yellow for the date/issue marker only

### R02 — るるぶハワイ'25 Kailua area spread

- Product preview: https://books.rakuten.co.jp/rb/17866285/
- Type: destination feature spread

Observed mechanisms:
- a wide lead landscape establishes the area before details are introduced
- the map is not a separate utility panel; it is integrated into the story
- food, beach, shopping, and access information use different image scales according to importance
- location letters, numbers, and short captions create scanability
- white background and light local motifs keep dense content from becoming a dashboard
- borders and decorative strokes are localized rather than applied to every module

Adopt:
- lead photo first, map/route second, details third
- large/medium/small photo hierarchy
- concise location markers shared between map and articles
- direct text and rules before full cards
- local motifs as low-contrast atmosphere, not as foreground stickers

Reject:
- copying the exact map, letter markers, border style, or article arrangement
- repeating every attraction as the same rounded card

V6 application:
- memory-spot page uses one lead destination plus three supporting stops
- route labels connect photo captions and map nodes
- each destination gets one meaningful category marker, not multiple badges

### R03 — まっぷる ハワイ'26 island overview

- Product page: https://ec.sp-mapple.jp/products/9784398299284
- Type: orientation / overview spread

Observed mechanisms:
- a simplified central map acts as the anchor
- circular and rectangular photographs are mixed to avoid grid monotony
- bright color differentiates topics, but the map and headline remain dominant
- information is grouped by traveller question: where, what, why
- small illustrations and arrows carry navigation meaning

Adopt:
- use diagrams/maps only when they answer a clear question
- vary image shape according to role, not randomly
- connect overview labels to specific narrative points

Reject:
- using many unrelated speech bubbles
- adding arrows or icons that do not help navigation

V6 application:
- the relationship/history page can use a travel-route metaphor with a clear chronological direction
- supporting memories may use circles only for details, not all photographs

### R04 — ハレ旅 ハワイ map spread

- Publisher article: https://note.com/asahi_books/n/ne68ea735ea1f
- Type: calm destination-planning spread

Observed mechanisms:
- muted map field gives the eye a calm base
- repeated destination nodes remain readable because they share a clear template and spacing system
- hand-drawn illustrations soften the page without overpowering information
- generous internal margins separate text from map lines
- limited palette produces a quieter, more premium travel tone

Adopt:
- use a calm base field for dense route/history information
- repeat only genuinely repeated information structures
- use illustrations as guides or emotional punctuation
- protect body copy from decorative line crossings

Reject:
- over-repeating identical cards in non-repeated content
- using illustration merely to fill empty space

V6 application:
- history spread uses a pale aqua/sand ground with a controlled route line
- dates and memories follow one repeated micro-template
- one or two hand-drawn accents maximum

### R05 — まっぷる ホノルル'27 Waikiki map

- Product page: https://ec.sp-mapple.jp/products/9784398218728
- Type: illustrated map / area guide

Observed mechanisms:
- coastline and roads provide a strong geographic skeleton
- a vertical photo strip creates a second information lane without breaking the map
- practical data is grouped into one restrained box
- warm paper tones prevent tropical blue from becoming visually cold
- small illustrations humanize the map while the information remains primary

Adopt:
- establish one geographic or narrative skeleton
- keep practical information in one coherent module
- combine tropical blue with warm paper/sand neutrals

Reject:
- decorative maps that cannot be followed
- scattering practical data across several unrelated badges

V6 application:
- back cover may use a simple departure-to-wedding route or closing itinerary
- practical date/location information stays in one small module

## Cross-reference synthesis

### What creates the immediate travel-guide feeling

1. A destination is identifiable before body text is read.
2. One photograph or map owns the spread.
3. Supporting modules have visibly different importance.
4. Labels, numbers, captions, and routes help navigation.
5. Color is used as a system, not as independent decoration.
6. Dense information is balanced by calm photo or map areas.
7. Small details reward inspection but do not replace hierarchy.

### What creates the Hawaii/resort feeling without cliché overload

- ocean horizon or resort architecture as the primary environment
- warm white, sand, pale aqua, deep ocean blue
- coral or hibiscus pink as a controlled accent
- sunlight, linen, flowers, glass, water reflection, and natural materials
- one or two palm/flower signals, not repeated tropical clip art
- sunset used for emotional closing rather than every page
- hospitality details: terrace, table setting, hotel corridor, pool edge, invitation, luggage, lei texture

### What creates the wedding identity

- date and location as issue information
- couple story framed as a journey
- profile and Q&A treated as editorial interviews
- memories treated as destinations
- Friends & Family treated as local voices or travel companions
- back cover ends with gratitude and onward-journey language

## V6 structural direction

### Outer concept A — Resort Panorama

- full-bleed or near-full-bleed Waikiki/resort panorama
- stable masthead at top
- one original warm-white title field
- three supporting cover lines placed directly on calm image areas or minimal color bands
- small circular or stamp-like secondary image only if it adds a distinct story
- back cover uses one sunset dinner image and a quiet closing itinerary

Strength:
- strongest immediate destination impact
- easiest to read at thumbnail size

Risk:
- can become a generic resort brochure if editorial details are too sparse

### Outer concept B — Editorial Resort Collage

- dominant hotel/ocean image occupying roughly two-thirds
- two supporting images with deliberately different ratios
- one vertical destination strip or issue line
- text integrated through alignment and rules, not multiple floating cards
- back cover uses Friends & Family as three unequal candid modules around one closing image

Strength:
- stronger magazine density and wedding storytelling

Risk:
- can become scrapbook-like if rotations, tape, and stickers are overused

### Inside direction

Profile/Q&A:
- one large profile atmosphere image; second profile smaller and differently cropped
- interview text directly on the page with rules and pull quotes
- maximum one full background panel for the main Q&A

History:
- calm map/route field
- chronology is the primary alignment logic
- one lead memory photo plus supporting milestones

Memory spots:
- one lead destination
- three supporting destinations with shared map markers
- mixed photo ratios but repeated caption relationship

## V6 color direction

Base:
- warm white / ivory
- sand beige

Primary environmental colors:
- deep ocean blue
- clear aqua

Accent:
- coral or hibiscus pink

Micro-accent:
- sunshine yellow used only for date, rank, or one key marker

Body text:
- deep navy or charcoal rather than pure black where contrast permits

Prohibited:
- gradients used as a default background
- cyan + coral + yellow at equal intensity everywhere
- every section assigned a separate saturated color

## V6 typography direction

- Japanese display: one friendly but authoritative high-impact face
- Japanese text: highly readable gothic or modern rounded gothic, not overly cute
- English: secondary atmosphere/category role only
- title scale should do more work than weight/shadow
- body, caption, and microcopy must survive actual-size print
- issue date and numerals use one consistent numeral system

## Quantitative design limits for the first concepts

These are starting constraints, not permanent laws:

- dominant cover photograph: at least 55% of front-cover visual area
- supporting cover-line groups: 2–4
- full rounded cards on front cover: 0–2
- badges/stickers with semantic roles: maximum 3
- dominant spread stories: 1
- supporting stories: 2–3
- independent accent colors per spread: maximum 3 plus neutrals
- one subtraction attempt required in every QA pass

## Research conclusion

V6 should not imitate the surface of one Rurubu issue. It should reproduce the professional editorial behavior found across multiple travel guides:

- a strong destination promise
- unequal information hierarchy
- useful navigation devices
- controlled color
- Japanese type discipline
- photo-led storytelling
- dense-but-edited detail
