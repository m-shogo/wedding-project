# 2026-08-12 — DE dense editorial lesson

## Reusable lesson

When a Rurubu-style spread already has a strong photo hierarchy but still reads like a clean profile sheet, **do not solve density by adding more cards, stickers, shadows, or rounded containers**. First create stronger editorial compression with one section-defining anchor, deliberately unequal Japanese type scale, and a small number of thin color rules that explain hierarchy.

DE `894:2` proved this on the inside Q&A: one magenta section bar establishes the article boundary; Q1 becomes the dominant human-interest pull quote through number/question/answer scale; Q2 and Q3 become compact secondary notes with cyan/yellow rules. The existing photos and native semantic text remain intact.

## Why it worked

- The page gains a clear reading sequence without introducing UI geometry.
- Scale relationships become materially different instead of three equal modules.
- Color acts as navigation, not decoration.
- The print page becomes denser while retaining negative space where it supports legibility.
- Native text remains editable and image crops/hashes remain unchanged.

## Guardrails

- Re-check actual-size text collisions whenever typography is enlarged or columns compressed.
- A color bar must establish a section or hierarchy; do not add one as filler.
- Do not use layout polish to disguise a bad dominant photo. The outer cover Q60 quality gate remains independent.
- If enlarging a weak raster makes its artifacts more visible, reject the experiment even if the composition feels more energetic. DC `891:2` is the recorded example.
