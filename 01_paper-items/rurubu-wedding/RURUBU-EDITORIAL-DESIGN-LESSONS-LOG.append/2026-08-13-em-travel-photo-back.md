# Rurubu editorial lesson — EM travel-photo back

## Lesson

A technically good dominant photograph can still be the wrong editorial signal. For Rurubu-like wedding travel editorial, a large object flat-lay may read as stationery, lifestyle commerce, or generic wedding stock even when it is attractive. If the page is supposed to read as travel information, a believable destination/landscape field can carry more genre identity than additional labels or decorative density.

## Applied experiment

EM `1094:2` replaced EK's dominant back-cover flat-lay with the already accepted V5 coast photograph, enlarged as the main field. Existing support food/travel photographs were overlapped at unequal scale and slight rotation. Japanese title and friends heading were placed directly on the image with solid native type. No new UI card, shadow, gradient, or external binary was added.

## Evidence

500px thumbnail, 1200px whole-reading, and natural-size front/back inspections favored EM. Live structure remained editable: 36 visible native text nodes, six visible IMAGE fills, zero same-parent text intersections, zero bounded safe-area text risks, fold preserved. EM Review snapshot is `1096:2`; EK `1081:2` is hidden rollback.

## Reuse rule

Same-version accepted imagery may be reused to test stronger composition when provenance is known, but it must be reported as reuse rather than generation/adoption of a new master. The Q60 front-cover lifecycle remains independent and open.

## Next use

When a spread remains 'too ugly' after multiple typography/layout passes, inspect whether the largest image is sending the wrong genre signal. Replace or recrop the dominant image before adding more decoration.
