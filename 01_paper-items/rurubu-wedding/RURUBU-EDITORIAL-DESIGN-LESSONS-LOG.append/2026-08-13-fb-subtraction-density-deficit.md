# FB — subtraction can expose a density deficit — 2026-08-13

## Lesson
Removing a repeated photograph is not automatically an editorial improvement. In a dense Japanese travel-magazine composition, every image also carries visual mass, directional energy, and page closure. Subtraction must be judged by the new hierarchy it creates, not by the cleanliness of the deletion itself.

## Applied in FB
FA repeated the accepted coast image on both back and front. FB tested a clean subtraction on a safe duplicate:
- hid the front coast fragment only
- enlarged Feature 02 and its existing native caption group
- preserved the exact secondary Yokohama Q60 destination postcard
- added no new external image, generated asset, rounded card, generic shadow, or gradient

At 500 px whole-item scale, the experiment exposed a large lower-right cream dead area. The remaining images did not carry enough mass to close the cover. FB was therefore rejected rather than promoted merely because it reduced asset repetition.

## Reusable rule
Before subtracting a photograph from a dense editorial cover, identify what will replace its three functions: area coverage, directional eye movement, and semantic destination support. If those functions cannot be supplied by a higher-confidence existing image or stronger typography at both thumbnail and actual-size scales, preserve the current Best and reject the subtraction.

## Next use
When an accepted cover feels repetitive, test alternative scale or crop of the remaining photographs only when the resulting page can be closed without dead area. A cleaner asset list is not more important than a stronger magazine silhouette.
