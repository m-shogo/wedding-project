# 2026-08-11 — BH/BI editorial lessons

1. A narrow asymmetrical paper strip can outperform a full-width lower panel on a travel-magazine cover because it preserves dominant-photo ownership while still carrying story hierarchy.
2. Supporting photo overlaps should cross the photo/paper seam deliberately; keeping every photo inside its own region recreates card/grid behavior.
3. `01 > 02 > 03` hierarchy should be visible at thumbnail scale through materially different number/headline sizes, not merely color changes.
4. Staggered timelines become more editorial when major milestones use larger years and secondary events occupy smaller offset beats, but event strings must be checked at actual-size to prevent wraps.
5. Structure QA should end at zero accidental same-parent text collisions even when some visual overlap is intentional. BH exposed a feature-number collision; BI required repeated bounding-box repair before zero.
6. For dense Japanese editorial compositions, use screenshot QA and programmatic bounding boxes together. Thumbnail judgement alone misses numerical/date wraps and small collisions.
7. A verified Drive master is not a Figma asset until its image hash is actually placed and visually inspected. Named staging frames are not sufficient evidence; the in-file Q60 audit found only the known temporary hash.
8. When the official external upload path fails before binary transfer with the same DNS fingerprint, switch to another visual target or in-file audit rather than burning the run on retries.
