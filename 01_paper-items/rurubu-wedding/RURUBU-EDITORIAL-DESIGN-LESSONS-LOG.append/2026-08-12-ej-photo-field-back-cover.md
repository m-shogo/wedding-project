# 2026-08-12 — EJ photo-field back-cover lesson

A magazine back cover becomes stronger when it is treated as an image field rather than a stack of modules. EJ proved that expanding one accepted photo to full bleed, crossing its lower edge with only two unequal support photos, and concentrating chronology into one cream 3×2 information field gives a clearer travel-editorial silhouette than multiple independent photo/timeline cards.

Keep these reusable rules:

- Prefer one dominant photographic surface plus one compact information surface over several equal-height sections.
- Support photos should differ materially in scale and angle; equal cards immediately reintroduce UI/dashboard rhythm.
- Large Japanese display type can sit directly on photography when contrast is genuinely verified at actual size; do not add a dark box by default.
- Subtraction must inspect nested descendants, not only obvious top-level decorations. Tiny inherited `FRIENDS` tabs survived the first pass and were visible only in render QA.
- Collision=0 is not print QA. The first EJ actual-size render still exposed lower-row/footer crowding, so rendered bottom-edge inspection remains mandatory.
- Preserve provenance boundaries: a visually plausible existing Figma hero is not evidence that the Drive Q60 asset was placed. Exact asset identity remains OPEN until node/image-hash evidence closes the lifecycle.
