# 2026-08-12 — DK: large Japanese type should do more work than headline cards

## Lesson
A travel-information magazine cover can become more authentic without adding more decoration if the hierarchy is rebuilt around native Japanese type. When a strong full-bleed photograph already exists, a broad headline rectangle often reads like a digital banner. A much larger headline placed directly on the photograph, supported by a narrow slanted accent rule, can create more editorial energy while preserving photo dominance.

Unequal supporting photographs should act as factual/editorial anchors, not a uniform gallery. One irregular paper fragment is enough for a major feature when secondary stories are allowed to sit directly on photography. Actual-size QA remains mandatory: DK initially allowed feature 03 typography to collide with the tilted support photo even though the overall composition looked acceptable at smaller scale.

DK `945:2` passed thumbnail, whole-spread, actual-size, and structure QA after that collision was repaired. The clean-room comparison also included live `939:2` and `942:2`; their denser collage/module approaches were preserved but not selected because they weakened headline dominance or moved back toward modular cards.

## Reuse rule
Before adding a new color block to a Rurubu-like cover, ask whether the same hierarchy can be achieved with larger native Japanese type, a short editorial rule, a purposeful crop, or stronger overlap. Prefer those print-native devices first. Keep one clear dominant photograph, allow secondary photos to vary materially in size/angle, and reject any state where supporting imagery forces text into collisions or creates equal-card rhythm.
