# 2026-08-12 — DJ: photography should become the page substrate, not just the hero

## Lesson

A cover can have a large photograph and still read like a poster if the information area begins as a separate broad rectangle beneath it. For an energetic Japanese travel-information magazine silhouette, strong photography can work better as the page substrate itself: native Japanese type, a bounded irregular paper fragment, factual inset photography, and unequal secondary images should interlock with that photographic field rather than start a second modular zone.

When the strongest available photograph is atmospheric rather than exact destination evidence, semantic honesty is mandatory. Label the atmospheric image as travel memory (or an equivalent role), keep exact destination evidence in a clearly separate factual anchor, and never imply that the atmosphere image depicts the named destination.

Subtraction remains the first move. A broad cream field can become another form of empty UI/poster geometry even without rounded corners. If actual-size QA shows dead paper area, shrink the field before adding decoration. Structure QA must also follow visual QA because direct-on-photo typography can create hidden text-box intersections that are not obvious in screenshots.

DJ `933:2` demonstrated the pattern: full-bleed verified travel-memory photography, native Japanese masthead and editorial type, one reduced 252px irregular cream feature patch, small tilted Yokohama proxy, unequal support photo, and zero same-parent text intersections after repairing one detected collision. Thumbnail, whole-spread, and actual-size QA all passed before promotion.

## Reuse rule

For future Rurubu/V6 concepts, ask whether the page is composed *on top of the photograph* or merely *next to the photograph*. If it is still divided into hero/photo zone plus broad information rectangle, try making verified photography the substrate and rebuild only the minimum print-native anchors on top. Preserve factual/atmospheric semantics separately and reject any state that improves density by reintroducing card-grid geometry.
