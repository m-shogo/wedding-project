## 0. Why this reset exists

V6 has become structurally clean and much less UI-like, but it was improved through a long sequence of local repairs. The next versions must not be V6 with more decoration.

The new goal is to learn how professional graphic designers, editorial designers and book designers think before placing objects in Figma, then make two materially different clean-room systems:

- V7: high-energy Hawaii travel-guide editorial
- V8: book-designer-led travel monograph / guide hybrid

V6 remains the verified comparator and rollback source. Its geometry is not the default starting point.

## 1. Professional research sources

This study extracts principles only. It does not copy commercial logos, exact spreads, proprietary illustrations, text, or page geometry.

### 1.1 Pentagram — publication design as theme + variation

Sources:
- https://www.pentagram.com/editorial-design
- https://www.pentagram.com/work/citizen
- https://www.pentagram.com/work/stern/story
- https://www.pentagram.com/work/netflix-queue

Observed:
- a publication needs a recurring framework, but each story should unfold differently;
- strong magazines use a flexible grid rather than repeating one card/module;
- pacing across pages matters: dramatic photography, dynamic typography, quiet interludes and dense service pages can coexist;
- image scale is deliberately unequal;
- section openers act as anchors in the sequence;
- a grid should organize complexity without becoming visible as UI.

Adopt:
- design a six-spread reading sequence, not six isolated screenshots;
- maintain a hidden grid and recurring folio/navigation language while allowing different story compositions;
- give every spread a named editorial role and tempo;
- use dramatic scale differences in photography and Japanese type.

Reject:
- copying Pentagram project typography, logos or exact grids;
- treating “flexible grid” as random rotation or arbitrary collage.

### 1.2 Book designer Jun Kawana — read the content before styling the object

Source:
- https://note.morisawa.co.jp/n/n66297c30a3c0

Observed:
- book design begins by reading the manuscript and hearing the editor/author’s intent;
- body format depends on volume, reading effort and the nature of the content;
- a publication requires system design, but the system serves the content;
- each book can be treated as having its own design personality.

Adopt:
- define the editorial responsibility of each Rurubu WEDDING spread before art direction;
- V7 and V8 must have different personalities, not just palettes;
- do not invent a graphic device until the content role demands it.

Rurubu-specific translation:
- profile = intimacy / identity;
- story = emotional narrative;
- chronology = sequence / memory;
- memory spots = destination-guide utility;
- cafe/table = service + lifestyle;
- 1DAY plan = actionable travel planning;
- outer = promise / cover hook / closing summary.

### 1.3 Book designer Asami Sato — type creates character, production remains collaborative

Sources:
- https://note.morisawa.co.jp/n/n49d9c267aec1
- https://note.morisawa.co.jp/n/n8010dfc150c0
- https://note.morisawa.co.jp/n/n84ac693d8b7c

Observed:
- typography can intentionally balance readability against expressive character;
- tension can come from contrast between illustration/photo mood and type personality;
- book production is collaborative, so future correction/editability matters.

Adopt:
- do not flatten mutable Japanese copy into decoration;
- choose headline/body/caption personalities intentionally instead of “one bold font everywhere”;
- retain native text where facts or wording can change;
- fixed visual support may be composed/generated separately when that improves craft.

### 1.4 Japanese composition — professional line breaking is not optional

Sources:
- https://helpx.adobe.com/jp/indesign/using/composing-cjk-characters.html
- https://helpx.adobe.com/jp/indesign/using/layout-grids.html
- https://helpx.adobe.com/jp/illustrator/desktop/design-with-text/fonts-and-scripts/mojikumi-kinsoku-overview.html

Observed:
- Japanese composition manages line-start/line-end forbidden characters, punctuation, mixed Latin/Japanese spacing and line rhythm;
- a layout grid is a compositional system, not merely equal boxes;
- print typography requires actual-size inspection.

Adopt:
- V7/V8 use explicit display / section / article / body / caption-micro roles;
- manually check punctuation, brackets, dates and Japanese/Latin joins at actual size;
- treat baseline rhythm as a hidden system even when image placement breaks columns;
- rerun realistic long-copy stress after material movement or type changes.

### 1.5 Current Japanese Hawaii travel-guide category

Sources:
- JTB Publishing Rurubu Hawaii / Honolulu listings and current catalogue:
  - https://books.jtbpublishing.co.jp/search?series=1226
  - https://books.jtbpublishing.co.jp/book/60001-202511145535-000/
  - https://books.jtbpublishing.co.jp/e-book/60001-202506145525-000/

Observed category grammar:
- many photographs, practical information and maps/model courses are core product value;
- Hawaii issues emphasize beach, activity, nature, sweets, dining, shopping and local towns;
- cover refreshes and strong feature framing coexist with high service density.

Adopt:
- V7 must contain both emotional Hawaii energy and useful guide information;
- do not make a beautiful resort brochure with no service layer;
- build image-led beats plus concise utility microcopy.

Reject:
- official logo reproduction;
- direct cover/page tracing;
- proprietary copy/illustration/map imitation;
- treating tropical flowers/stickers as sufficient “Hawaii-ness.”

## 3. V7 concept charter — HAWAII POP EDITORIAL / ISLAND GUIDE SPECIAL

Personality:
- energetic, optimistic, useful, colorful, immediate;
- a Japanese Hawaii travel-guide special made for a wedding, not a luxury resort brochure.

Editorial promise:
- “ページをめくるたび、次に行きたい場所・食べたいもの・ふたりの話が現れる。”

### V7 visual grammar

- high color energy, but concentrate color in editorial anchors rather than painting every field;
- dominant Hawaii-specific photography should eventually occupy large page areas;
- large Japanese headlines are primary; English is category/atmosphere only;
- irregular image ratios and controlled overlap;
- short service labels, practical notes and guide information create scan density;
- fixed non-variable decorations are section-specific composed PNG assets when needed;
- mutable titles, facts, captions and personal copy remain native text;
- photo roles remain replaceable masks/crops.

Palette direction:
- sun yellow / coral-pink / turquoise / cobalt / warm cream / deep navy;
- do not use every color at equal intensity on every spread.

Grid:
- hidden 12-column system as an organizing base;
- headline and image beats may break the grid intentionally;
- no repeated 2×3 card grid.

Pacing proposal:
1. Outer: loud promise / dominant destination image / fast feature cues.
2. Profile-Q&A: personal and playful, slightly calmer.
3. Story-chronology: cinematic photo-led narrative.
4. Memory spots: densest destination-guide service spread.
5. Cafe-table: food/lifestyle feature, high sensory image energy.
6. 1DAY: practical model course with dynamic stop hierarchy.

V7 non-goals:
- no fake real-Rurubu logo reproduction;
- no generic “Hawaii = hibiscus everywhere” treatment;
- no pasted sticker set repeated across every spread;
- no dashboard/card grid;
- no false Hawaii photography claim from current Yokohama dummy assets.

## 4. V8 concept charter — BOOK DESIGNER EDITION / TRAVEL MONOGRAPH + GUIDE

Personality:
- authored, tactile, intelligent, calmer but not empty;
- a book designer’s travel essay that still contains guide utility.

Editorial promise:
- “旅の記憶を読む本。必要な情報は静かに、写真とことばは大胆に。”

### V8 visual grammar

- fewer accent colors; 2–3 inks/accents plus paper tone;
- stronger typographic rhythm and margins;
- full-bleed photo essay pages alternate with denser utility pages;
- more visible baseline/column discipline than V7;
- one story premise per spread;
- fixed texture/decor can be composed raster, but only when tied to the spread premise;
- avoid generic minimal-luxury whitespace: quiet space must create pacing or reading focus;
- folio/navigation becomes a book-level rhythm, not decoration.

Grid:
- rigorous underlying modular grid with consistent body-copy measures;
- large photos/headlines deliberately cross or occupy multiple modules;
- captions and folios align to a baseline family.

Pacing proposal:
1. Outer: high-contrast typographic cover with one decisive image idea.
2. Profile-Q&A: intimate reader-led opening, restrained visual system.
3. Story-chronology: photo essay with a strong sequence and fewer simultaneous facts.
4. Memory spots: controlled service page with clear map/metadata logic.
5. Cafe-table: tactile food essay + compact guide appendix.
6. 1DAY: timetable/editorial index hybrid, information-dense but typographically ordered.

V8 non-goals:
- not a beige luxury wedding brochure;
- not a simple “V7 but quieter” recolor;
- not a sparse art book that loses travel-guide usefulness;
- no over-designed micro labels merely to show design skill.

## 5. V7 vs V8 must differ structurally

| Dimension | V7 | V8 |
|---|---|---|
| primary energy | pop travel-guide | authored book/editorial |
| color | high, selective | restrained, ink-like |
| image handling | collage / overlap / cutout rhythm | decisive full-bleed / modular photo essays |
| typography | giant playful Japanese display | disciplined Japanese hierarchy with expressive exceptions |
| utility | visible, fast scanning | compact, ordered, reference-like |
| decoration | section-specific composed accents | texture/material/folio system only when meaningful |
| pacing | energetic alternation | deliberate chapter-like sequence |
| desired feeling | “今すぐ旅したい” | “この本を取っておきたい” |

## 6. Professional comparison rubric

Score 1–5 at thumbnail, reading and actual-size scales.

1. editorial premise clarity
2. whole-book pacing
3. travel-guide authenticity
4. wedding identity
5. visual hierarchy
6. photography art direction
7. Japanese typography / line breaks
8. service-information usefulness
9. anti-template / anti-UI quality
10. section-to-section variation within one system
11. editability / photo replacement tolerance
12. print plausibility

Critical fail regardless of total:
- copied logo/page;
- fake destination truth;
- unreadable actual-size text;
- mutable facts baked into generated artwork;
- image role exceeds source quality;
- text/crop/fold/safe-area collision;
- a V8 that is merely a color-muted V7;
- a V7 that is merely a colorful V6.

## 7. V7/V8 Figma build rules

Before build:
- preserve all V6 preferred frames unchanged as comparison authority;
- create V7 and V8 on a separate Rurubu working surface;
- use clean-room geometry rather than duplicate-and-polish as the default;
- existing V6 photos may be used only as clearly documented structural/dummy mask content until role-correct V7/V8 photography is available.

Figma authoring:
- native mutable Japanese text;
- replaceable photo roles/masks;
- no SVG in Rurubu production under current item authority;
- fixed composed decoration, if adopted, uses transparent PNG and remains separate from mutable copy;
- simple rules/fields may stay native only when they perform real binding/contrast/physical function;
- long-copy stress after material type/position changes;
- review whole, page and actual size.

## 8. Image-generation strategy after structural proof

Do not generate a generic “Hawaii magazine background.”

Generate only a diagnosed role, for example:
- V7 cover Hawaii destination hero;
- V7 section-specific editorial support cluster;
- V8 tactile paper/map texture;
- V8 photo-essay transition asset.

Every brief must include:
- exact aspect ratio;
- focal point;
- text-safe zone;
- lighting/lens character;
- travel-mag editorial role;
- print detail target;
- explicit negatives for fake signs/text, warped architecture, plastic surfaces and stock-AI polish.

Then follow the full asset lifecycle before counting adoption.
