# Lesson — A wide history image must carry narrative direction

Date: 2026-08-04
Applies to: Rurubu V5, V6, and later editorial timeline/history spreads
Status: `PROTOTYPED → VERIFIED_FOR_DERIVATIVE / PROJECT_RULE_NOT_YET_PROMOTED`

## Context

The V5 history lead role uses a very wide `678 × 280` frame. A source image can be attractive but still fail this role when its wide crop loses either destination context or the sense of movement needed by the chronology.

## Observed opportunity

The waterfront source contained several possible stories: sunset sky, mountains, water, city buildings, two distant walkers, and a lit boardwalk. Cropping only to maximize scenery would make a generic panorama. Cropping only to maximize the path would lose the destination atmosphere.

## Hypothesis

For a history/chronology lead, the strongest crop should preserve a directional environmental line that visually supports time moving forward, while retaining enough contextual scenery to establish place and emotional tone.

## Test

Five crops at the exact target ratio were compared. The accepted crop retained:

- the boardwalk and handrail as a left-to-right directional line
- distant figures as scale and journey cues without false identity
- mountain, water, and skyline context
- sufficient sky and sunset for emotional breathing room

Figma placement is not yet complete, so the lesson is verified only at derivative level. It must not be promoted into the project-wide knowledge base until live spread screenshot QA confirms that the directional crop strengthens the chronology beside actual text and route elements.

## Reusable candidate principle

When selecting a wide image for a history or route spread, judge more than subject visibility. Prefer a crop whose roads, rails, coastlines, paths, gaze, or architectural perspective reinforce the intended reading direction. Preserve place context and quiet space; do not reduce the image to a decorative banner.

## Failure guard

Directional lines are not automatically good. Reject crops where the line points out of the page, drives attention into the fold, removes the key landmark, creates awkward subject cropping, or overpowers nearby chronology labels.
