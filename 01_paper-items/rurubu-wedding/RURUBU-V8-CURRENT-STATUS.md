## Latest professional learning — Cafe L

New research focused on **pacing through scale contrast rather than automatically filling white space**.

Eye Magazine's Aperture redesign analysis notes that stronger editorial pacing came from deliberately modulating picture scale, white space and story tempo instead of maintaining an even visual flow. magCulture's review of The Gourmand #11 shows that an unfussy text-led page can be convincing inside a publication that also has much more flamboyant visual pages. David Lane's The Gourmand interview reinforces that text and imagery need their own editorial ideas and decisive scale rather than default middle-weight treatment.

Local hypothesis: Cafe/Table I was structurally correct but still felt like a refined wireframe because most visible elements occupied similar medium-weight scales and the two pages had nearly equal visual tempo. White space alone was not creating professional pacing.

Bounded test: Cafe L `2183:2` keeps the semantically weak generated TABLE_ESSAY hidden, promotes the meaningful left thesis `食べたものより、食卓を覚えている。` to a much larger type role, reduces the sensory lines to supporting scale, strengthens the right-page food/atmosphere headline, and uses larger but low-opacity semantic index text for `04` / `夜`. No cards, unrelated images, decorative English, shadows or gradients were added.
