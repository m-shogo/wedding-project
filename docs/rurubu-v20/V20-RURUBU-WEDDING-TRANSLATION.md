# V20 RURUBU WEDDING Translation Layer

Status: `CANONICAL_WEDDING_TRANSLATION / V20_OVERRIDE_WHEN_TRAVEL_GRAMMAR_OVERREACHES`

Purpose: ensure V20 is designed as **RURUBU WEDDING**, not merely as a travel magazine featuring a couple.

This layer sits above generic travel-magazine imitation. V20 may borrow the editorial behaviors of lively Japanese travel publishing, but every meaningful module must be translated into the wedding/couple experience.

## 0. Core identity

V20 is:

`JAPANESE TRAVEL-EDITORIAL BEHAVIOR`
× `REAL COUPLE STORY`
× `TODAY'S WEDDING EXPERIENCE`
× `KEEPSAKE VALUE`

It is NOT:
- a destination guide with wedding photos pasted in;
- a generic wedding profile book wearing travel icons;
- a normal timetable/menu booklet decorated with airplanes;
- a copied commercial Rurubu page.

The desired reaction is:

> “This feels like a real RURUBU-style wedding special issue made only for SHOGO & SHIORI.”

not:

> “This is a travel magazine.”

and not:

> “This is a standard wedding profile book with travel stickers.”

---

## 1. Wedding meaning outranks travel literalism

When travel-editorial vocabulary conflicts with wedding relevance, wedding meaning wins.

Translate instead of copying literal travel modules:

| Travel-magazine behavior | V20 wedding translation |
| --- | --- |
| destination feature | memory/travel destination tied to the couple |
| model course | couple story / journey / today’s experience |
| sightseeing BEST | things guests should notice about the couple or today |
| local highlight | couple-specific highlight / proposal / real-life detail |
| map / route | relationship/travel/arrival narrative |
| trip memo | grounded anecdote / memory note |
| editor’s pick | couple’s real favorite / wedding detail worth noticing |
| destination stamp | place/time marker grounded in real travel/wedding facts |
| guide article | readable couple story or wedding discovery block |
| hotel/access/transit | normally removed from seated-guest V20 |
| itinerary | only used when it tells story, not as logistics-first utility |
| final destination | today’s wedding / Yokohama / continuation of the couple’s journey |

Literal travel information that does not help a seated wedding guest understand, enjoy or remember the couple/event should be cut.

---

## 2. The wedding must remain visible on every spread

Every physical spread should contain at least one strong wedding/couple anchor.

Eligible anchors:
- real couple photography;
- names;
- a real relationship episode;
- proposal/marriage milestone;
- a real personal preference or habit;
- today’s wedding detail;
- real family/dog/candid context;
- wedding date/location metadata;
- a grounded note of thanks/continuation.

This does NOT mean writing “WEDDING” everywhere.

It means the editorial subject remains the two people and today’s celebration even when the surface language resembles travel publishing.

---

## 3. Page roles through a wedding lens

### P01 — Wedding Special Issue Cover
The cover promises:
- this is about SHOGO & SHIORI;
- today is special;
- travel is the editorial metaphor/theme;
- the couple, not a destination, is the hero.

A beach/sky/tropical image cannot visually overpower the identity of the couple and wedding issue.

### P02 — Meet the Couple
Not tourism profiles.
Show personality, contrast, habits, preferences and recognizable details that help guests know the two people.

### P03 — Their Story
The travel metaphor may support the flow, but the content is relationship narrative.
Do not turn it into a fake sightseeing timeline.

### P04–P05 — Memories as the Journey Peak
Travel destinations are real memories, not travel recommendations.
The emotional peak is the couple’s shared experience, especially proposal/marriage-relevant meaning where grounded.

### P06 — Off the Map
This intentionally breaks away from tourism and shows ordinary life, dogs, food, candid humor and personality.
This page is critical: it prevents V20 from becoming “a Hawaii/travel photo book.”

### P07 — Today’s Wedding Discovery
Travel-editorial discovery grammar is redirected into the physical wedding now surrounding the guest.
Only real/confirmed décor, table themes, cake, travel motifs, details or small discoveries are eligible.
This is not a timetable or arrival guide.

### P08 — Wedding Keepsake Closing
The close is gratitude + continuation.
Travel language may frame the ending, but the emotional subject is the marriage and guests who shared the day.

---

## 4. Wedding warmth without wedding-template clichés

V20 should feel celebratory, personal and warm without falling into generic wedding stationery language.

Avoid as default visual/copy shortcuts:
- endless hearts;
- script-font “Love” decoration;
- generic rings/doves/champagne icons;
- pale beige luxury whitespace as automatic elegance;
- “最高の思い出をありがとう” type filler;
- fake vows or fake quotes;
- bridal floral frames repeated everywhere;
- rose-gold wedding-template styling unrelated to the travel editorial system.

Wedding feeling should come primarily from:
- the real couple;
- their real history;
- today’s actual details;
- celebratory editorial hierarchy;
- warm personal copy;
- authentic photography;
- the opening-to-closing narrative arc.

---

## 5. Travel motif dosage

Airplanes, routes, passports, tickets, stamps, maps, destination labels and tropical objects are editorial vocabulary, not mandatory decoration.

Rule:

`TRAVEL MOTIF MUST SERVE COUPLE/WEDDING STORY OR READING FLOW.`

If removing an airplane/ticket/stamp changes no meaning and no hierarchy, it is probably filler.

Do not make every page use every travel motif.

Use different translations:
- route as relationship movement;
- ticket as feature vessel;
- stamp as memory locator;
- postcard as photo treatment;
- destination label as chapter marker;
- map only where actual geography or journey helps the story.

---

## 6. Wedding-specific content filter

Every candidate block should pass at least one:

1. Does it help a guest know SHOGO or SHIORI better?
2. Does it reveal a real shared memory?
3. Does it create a conversation topic at the table?
4. Does it help the guest notice/enjoy something happening around today’s wedding?
5. Does it add meaningful keepsake value after the wedding?
6. Does it strengthen the emotional journey from cover to closing?

If none apply, remove or replace it even if it looks travel-magazine-like.

---

## 7. Wedding-specific hierarchy rule

Travel scenery and generated editorial decoration must not become more important than the human subject across the publication.

Across the full book, the hierarchy should read roughly:

`THE TWO PEOPLE`
→ `THEIR STORY / MEMORIES`
→ `TODAY'S WEDDING`
→ `TRAVEL AS EDITORIAL LANGUAGE`
→ `DECORATION`.

A spectacular generated tropical cluster that steals attention from the couple is a failed asset.
A beautiful destination photo that makes the spread feel like tourism advertising rather than a personal memory requires rebalancing through crop, title, copy, other photos or support elements.

---

## 8. Wedding Special Issue test

Before promoting a page/spread, ask:

- If the travel icons disappeared, would the page still clearly belong to this couple?
- If the couple names disappeared, would it become a generic travel page? If yes, strengthen personal content.
- Is the hero today’s couple/story rather than the destination itself?
- Is there something a wedding guest can smile at, learn, discuss or notice today?
- Does the page contribute to the emotional wedding arc?
- Does it avoid duplicating logistics/menu/seating owned by other paper items?
- Does it still use high-energy Japanese travel-editorial behavior rather than generic wedding-template composition?

The target is a deliberate hybrid, not a compromise:

`RURUBU EDITORIAL ENERGY + WEDDING-SPECIFIC MEANING`.

---

## 9. Correction order when V20 feels “too travel”

Do not immediately add hearts or wedding icons.

Correct in this order:
1. page content/job;
2. couple/wedding anchor strength;
3. hero-photo choice/crop;
4. headline wording;
5. relationship between destination imagery and personal story;
6. today/keepsake relevance;
7. travel-motif dosage;
8. only then small celebratory accents.

---

## 10. Correction order when V20 feels “too wedding-template”

Do not simply add airplanes/stamps.

Correct in this order:
1. editorial feature promise;
2. title scale/silhouette;
3. photo inequality and crop;
4. asymmetric clusters;
5. stronger layer collision;
6. information-vessel variety;
7. page-edge tension;
8. useful micro discoveries;
9. page-turn tempo;
10. only then travel motifs.

---

## 11. Final doctrine

The strongest V20 page should be recognizable as wedding content because of **people, story, memory and today**, and recognizable as Rurubu-like editorial because of **hierarchy, density, photo behavior, title silhouette, clustering, collision, information vessels and reading fun**.

Wedding supplies the meaning.
Travel editorial supplies the storytelling language.
V20 supplies the original identity.
