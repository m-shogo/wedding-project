# V20 Rurubu Editorial Design Decode

Status: `CANONICAL_DESIGN_DECODE / CLEAN_ROOM_BEHAVIORAL_REFERENCE`

Purpose: explain the editorial behaviors that create the lively Japanese travel-guide feeling V20 is targeting, without copying the commercial Rurubu logo, exact layouts, proprietary artwork, page coordinates or trade-dress details.

This file is a behavioral/art-direction authority. It converts “make it more Rurubu-like” into reusable editorial decisions.

## 0. Core conclusion

The target feeling does **not** come from stickers, tropical icons or bright colors by themselves.

It is created by a system:

`EDITORIAL IDEA`
→ `CLEAR READER PROMISE`
→ `DOMINANT PHOTO / TITLE GESTURE`
→ `MULTI-SCALE INFORMATION HIERARCHY`
→ `ASYMMETRIC CLUSTERS`
→ `STRONG LAYER COLLISION`
→ `PURPOSE-BUILT INFORMATION VESSELS`
→ `MICRO DISCOVERIES`
→ `PAGE-TO-PAGE TEMPO`
→ `PRACTICAL READABILITY`

V20 must reproduce this behavior, not a commercial page.

---

# 1. EDITORIAL IDEA FIRST

Every page/spread needs one sentence that explains what the reader is being invited to enjoy.

Good examples of page jobs:
- “Meet the two people behind today’s wedding.”
- “This is the travel-memory peak.”
- “This is who they are when they are not travelling.”
- “Look around the room and discover today’s details.”

A page is weak when its only explanation is:
- “profile page”;
- “photo page”;
- “information page”;
- “cute page”.

The visual composition must dramatize the editorial idea.

---

# 2. READER PROMISE / FIRST READ

A strong page gives the reader a clear promise within roughly a three-second glance.

Usually this comes from one of:
- a dominant headline silhouette;
- one hero photo;
- a destination statement;
- one large feature vessel;
- one strong route/diagram gesture;
- one unusual crop/cutout.

If five objects compete equally, the page feels generated or templated.

Rule:
`ONE FIRST-READ EVENT > MANY EQUAL DECORATIONS`.

---

# 3. TITLE SILHOUETTE IS A GRAPHIC SHAPE

Large Japanese titles are not merely labels. They are page-defining visual masses.

Useful behaviors:
- large scale jump from title to body;
- multi-line irregular silhouette;
- deliberate line breaks;
- white/cream keyline;
- hard offset shadow;
- vertical mini-title attached to horizontal display type;
- short tab/ribbon connected to the title;
- partial overlap with photography;
- title entering/leaving the page edge;
- asymmetric placement rather than default centering.

Avoid:
- identical title band on every page;
- same title size everywhere;
- one centered heading above a tidy grid;
- decorative English larger than meaningful Japanese copy.

---

# 4. PHOTO HIERARCHY IS UNEQUAL

Travel editorial energy depends on image-weight inequality.

Use photo roles:
`HERO / EMOTION / PLACE / ACTION / DETAIL / LIFE / FUN / BRIDGE`.

Typical strong page behavior:
- one photo clearly dominates;
- 1–3 satellites support it;
- crop shapes and aspect ratios differ;
- some photos are partly hidden;
- some photos break containment;
- one detail image may be tiny because its job is discovery, not equality.

Avoid:
- four or six equal photo cards;
- identical radii and borders;
- matching rotations;
- forcing strong real photos into prebuilt geometry.

Frame geometry follows source behavior.

---

# 5. CONTROLLED DENSITY, NOT UNIFORM DENSITY

The target is high information energy with deliberate calm zones.

A strong page often contains:
- one dense cluster;
- one medium-density bridge;
- one calmer readable field;
- several micro discoveries.

Density should have a peak.

Avoid:
- equally filling every empty space;
- equal padding everywhere;
- making every page maximum density;
- using empty luxury whitespace without editorial reason.

V20 page-temperature rhythm remains:
- P01 maximum impact;
- P02 lively/person-focused;
- P03 calmer/story-led;
- P04–P05 peak;
- P06 loose/candid;
- P07 active discovery/information;
- P08 deliberate release.

---

# 6. FULL EDITORIAL COLLISION

Layer collision is a feature, not a defect.

Photography, title, route, frame, vessel, stamp, flower cluster and paper object may overlap deeply.

Useful depth patterns:
- title over photo;
- cutout subject in front of title;
- title behind another photo edge;
- route under one object and over another;
- paper field hiding a large part of a photo;
- stamp only partly visible;
- flower/leaf cluster entering from bleed and passing behind/above the hero;
- support photo covering a hero corner.

There is no global overlap-percentage target.

Gate:
`COLLISION MAY BE STRONG; READABILITY AND HIERARCHY MUST BE STRONGER.`

---

# 7. TEXT SUPPORT IS EDITORIAL ART DIRECTION

Busy pages remain readable because text is deliberately protected.

Text-support vocabulary:
- paper plate;
- irregular color slab;
- ribbon/band;
- printed label;
- keyline/outline;
- hard offset shadow;
- local contrast field;
- photo quiet zone;
- small postcard/article field;
- dark/light localized backing;
- selective texture reduction behind body copy.

The support itself should feel authored for the page.

Avoid repeated generic UI cards or glassmorphism.

---

# 8. INFORMATION VESSELS FORM AN ECOLOGY

A travel magazine does not need one universal component.

Use materially different vessels for materially different information jobs:
- FEATURE = burst / slab / large paper field;
- DESTINATION = stamp / locator / ticket shell;
- BODY STORY = stable article-paper field;
- CAPTION = edge tab / small paper note;
- PICK = strong label / marker stroke / framed callout;
- META = tiny strip / folio / print note;
- QUOTE or PERSONAL NOTE = handwritten paper scrap only when grounded;
- TODAY DISCOVERY = irregular observation badge / small numbered stop.

The family is unified by print DNA, color logic and typography—not identical silhouettes.

---

# 9. COLOR HAS JOBS

V20 does not distribute palette colors evenly.

Colors perform roles:
- dominant environment / travel energy;
- feature emphasis;
- destination distinction;
- text-support contrast;
- micro accent;
- calm paper field.

Photo-aware rule:
select final page/spread color weighting **after** the important real photo is chosen.

Use real-photo colors as input to generated asset direction when helpful.

Do not paste a fixed palette mechanically over every source photo.

---

# 10. REAL → GENERATED BRIDGE

To avoid the “real photo + unrelated AI decoration + unrelated internet asset” collage problem:

1. inspect the real hero/support photos;
2. identify source palette, light direction, visual weight, negative space and crop behavior;
3. inspect any licensed third-party material;
4. define the page’s `LINE / COLOR / TEXTURE / DEPTH / ERA` profile;
5. generate missing editorial artwork specifically to bridge those sources;
6. place it in Figma with the real photos;
7. reject or regenerate anything that still looks sourced from another publication/world.

Image generation is therefore not only “asset creation.”

It is a **cohesion bridge** between real memories and prepared editorial art.

---

# 11. INTERNET / THIRD-PARTY ASSET GATE

Before raw placement, verify source/license and evaluate:
- LINE — stroke character / outline behavior;
- COLOR — saturation, warmth, ink behavior;
- TEXTURE — clean vector vs paper/print vs hand-made;
- DEPTH — flat, hard-shadow, photographic, dimensional;
- ERA — contemporary travel editorial, retro print, wedding stationery, app/UI, etc.

If two or more dimensions materially conflict with V20, do not place raw.

Options:
- recolor/rework if the license allows;
- reduce it to reference only;
- replace it;
- generate a page-aware bridge asset.

A strong asset that breaks publication unity is still a rejected asset.

---

# 12. MICRO DISCOVERIES CREATE “MAGAZINE DEPTH”

Once hierarchy already works, add useful small discoveries:
- folio/page number;
- issue/date metadata;
- small photo credit-like role label where appropriate;
- EDITOR’S PICK / LOOK AROUND / TRIP MEMO;
- annotation arrow;
- tiny stamp fragment;
- destination node;
- mini caption;
- small “check!” style marker;
- compact editorial note.

Every micro element must answer:
`WHAT DOES THIS HELP THE READER NOTICE / UNDERSTAND / ENJOY?`

If the answer is “fills a gap,” remove it.

---

# 13. PAGE EDGE TENSION

The page should not always look like every element was safely centered inside a canvas.

Eligible edge behavior:
- photo cropped by bleed;
- headline partly exits the page;
- flower/leaf object enters from edge;
- ticket/paper fragment only partially appears;
- route starts off-page or leaves page;
- stamp clipped by trim;
- giant number/title touches or crosses safe visual boundaries while critical text remains safe.

Edge tension is controlled, not accidental clipping.

---

# 14. SPREAD EVENT > TWO GOOD PAGES

P02–P03, P04–P05 and P06–P07 are designed as physical events.

A spread needs:
- a dominant shared movement or contrast;
- page-to-page hierarchy;
- intentional fold behavior;
- different but complementary page silhouettes;
- at least one relationship across the gutter through route/color/photo gaze/typographic direction or negative space.

P04–P05 is the peak and may use the strongest continuous route/collision event.

Critical faces and essential body text stay clear of the fold.

---

# 15. PAGE TURN / PUBLICATION RHYTHM

The book should feel edited when pages turn.

Suggested emotional sequence:
`DEPARTURE`
→ `MEET THEM`
→ `READ THEIR STORY`
→ `TRAVEL PEAK`
→ `SEE REAL LIFE`
→ `LOOK AROUND TODAY`
→ `QUIET CONTINUATION`.

Do not repeat:
- same hero position;
- same title alignment;
- same photo count;
- same density peak;
- same vessel family;
- same dominant color;
- same overlap gesture;
across adjacent pages by default.

---

# 16. EDITORIAL COPY IS PART OF THE DESIGN

Rurubu-like energy is weakened by generic wedding-template prose.

Preferred copy behavior:
- short;
- specific;
- grounded;
- conversational but edited;
- occasionally playful;
- makes the reader want to look at a photo or ask the couple a question.

Avoid:
- generic “最高の思い出” language;
- fake quotes;
- invented facts;
- excessive decorative English;
- internal-schema language;
- overlong exposition when a strong photo can carry the story.

---

# 17. AUTHENTIC PERSONAL TRACE

If available and approved, small amounts of real human trace can strongly reduce synthetic feel:
- scanned handwritten words;
- genuine travel ticket/card/paper ephemera;
- real photographed signage/details from the trip;
- true personal mini-note.

Use sparingly.

Do not create fake “memory ephemera” and present it as real.

---

# 18. ORIGINAL PUBLICATION IDENTITY

V20 must not become a commercial-logo imitation.

Borrow behavioral principles only:
- exuberant Japanese travel editorial energy;
- clear reader promise;
- useful information density;
- asymmetric image hierarchy;
- strong title silhouettes;
- editorial layering;
- practical readability;
- page-turn variety.

Build original identity through:
- original masthead;
- SHOGO/SHIORI-specific copy;
- original page-number/folio treatment;
- original route character;
- original small editorial-note grammar;
- real personal photographs/memories;
- V20-specific color/crop decisions.

The desired reaction is:
“this feels like a real Japanese travel magazine made for these two,”
not:
“this is a copied commercial page.”

---

# 19. SIX-LENS PAGE REVIEW

Every serious decorated candidate is reviewed through:

1. `ART DIRECTOR` — clear editorial idea and memorable silhouette?
2. `EDITORIAL DESIGNER` — reading order, density and page job correct?
3. `BOOK DESIGNER` — does it improve the page-turn/spread sequence?
4. `JAPANESE TYPOGRAPHER` — type hierarchy, line breaks and punctuation intentional?
5. `PHOTO EDITOR` — source choice, crop, scale and image inequality doing real work?
6. `PRINT DESIGNER` — survives A5, bleed/fold, effective PPI, CMYK and actual-size reading?

A candidate can be attractive and still fail.

---

# 20. QUALITY CORRECTION ORDER

When the page feels weak, correct in this order:

1. page idea/job;
2. content selection;
3. hero/photo source;
4. hero/title scale;
5. title silhouette;
6. crop and image inequality;
7. reading path;
8. spread movement;
9. text support / calm zone;
10. full editorial collision and depth;
11. color job / photo cohesion;
12. vessel design;
13. typography details;
14. micro discoveries;
15. micro spacing.

Do not add stickers to repair steps 1–10.

---

# 21. “IS IT RURUBU-LIKE ENOUGH?” TEST

Do not ask only whether the page contains bright colors or travel icons.

Ask:

- Is there an immediate feature promise?
- Is one image/title gesture dominant?
- Are image weights clearly unequal?
- Is information dense but navigable?
- Are there multiple reading scales?
- Do visual clusters overlap and interlock?
- Are text-support objects authored rather than generic cards?
- Is useful information made fun to read?
- Are there small discoveries after the first read?
- Does the page differ from its neighbors?
- Does the spread feel like an event?
- Does it still read at A5 actual size?
- Does it look like one V20 publication when mixed with real photos and generated/third-party assets?

If several answers are “no,” adding more decorative motifs will not solve the problem.

---

# 22. Remaining empirical work

This design decode is strong enough to guide production, but it is not treated as mathematically complete.

Remaining validation must come from actual V20 pages:
- P01 serious cover variants with real photo behavior;
- P04–P05 complete travel spread;
- P06–P07 late-book rhythm;
- P08 closing relationship to P01;
- actual Japanese copy at A5 size;
- real-photo color-cohesion trial;
- printed proof.

Production evidence may refine this document when a durable pattern is discovered.

Do not create more abstract design rules merely to feel complete.
