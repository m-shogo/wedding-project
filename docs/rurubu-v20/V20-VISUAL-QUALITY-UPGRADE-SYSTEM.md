# V20 Visual Quality Upgrade System

Status: `CANONICAL_QUALITY_REFINEMENT`

Purpose: raise the visible quality of the finished printed booklet. This is not a license to add endless rules, decorative filler, or governance. It defines the quality levers that should be tested in real page/spread production.

The governing idea is:

> Do not ask what else can be added. Ask what single change would make this look most like a professionally art-directed Japanese travel magazine at A5 print size.

---

## 1. Quality loop

For every serious page/spread batch:

`REFERENCE / REAL SOURCE REVIEW`
→ `BUILD 2–3 SERIOUS OPTIONS WHEN HIGH-IMPACT`
→ `PLACE IN FIGMA`
→ `VIEW PAGE + PHYSICAL SPREAD`
→ `IDENTIFY SINGLE LARGEST QUALITY GAP`
→ `CORRECT STRUCTURE / HIERARCHY / CROP / TYPOGRAPHY / COLOR`
→ `RECHECK`
→ `ONLY THEN MICRODETAILS`

Possible best corrections include:
- remove decoration;
- enlarge HERO photo;
- enlarge or reshape title;
- merge small containers;
- increase photo-size contrast;
- move text deeper into photography with better support;
- reduce weak copy;
- replace a weak source image;
- change crop behavior;
- increase local contrast;
- change page temperature.

Adding more stickers is never the default correction.

---

## 2. Real-photo cohesion / color direction

Real photos may come from different cameras, years, light and environments. V20 should not force them through one blunt filter, but the complete booklet should still feel photographed/edited for one publication.

### Publication-level grade philosophy

- natural skin tone is higher priority than palette matching;
- ocean/sky may stay lively but must not become synthetic cyan;
- protect dark detail so CMYK conversion does not turn shadows into flat black;
- keep white/cream highlights sympathetic to the PAPER/WARM_CREAM editorial system;
- interior/night photos may be warmer or darker than tropical photos, but should share contrast and black-point behavior;
- avoid obvious preset/filter signatures;
- preserve original source separately;
- use non-destructive corrections where practical.

### Evaluate at three scales

1. thumbnail / spread unity;
2. reading size / skin + color balance;
3. A5 actual-size / shadow detail, noise, edge quality, print risk.

Do not claim one grade preset as canonical until enough materially different real photos have been tested.

---

## 3. Photo-treatment variety

A travel magazine needs theme + variation, not one photo component repeated everywhere.

Valid treatment families include:
- full bleed;
- strong rectangular crop with white or colored keyline;
- postcard object;
- polaroid-like object when editorially useful;
- extreme landscape strip;
- extreme portrait crop;
- circle / oval;
- irregular crop;
- selective subject cutout;
- frame-behind / subject-in-front depth treatment.

Rules:
- frame geometry follows strong source behavior;
- not every photo rotates;
- not every photo receives tape;
- not every photo becomes a polaroid;
- cutouts are accents, not the default;
- one page may intentionally use fewer treatment types if that creates stronger hierarchy.

---

## 4. Japanese typography as visual structure

Typography is a primary quality lever, not a finishing layer.

### Type roles

- `DISPLAY` — page-defining title / masthead;
- `FEATURE` — major feature heading;
- `ARTICLE` — section/story heading;
- `BODY` — readable Japanese prose;
- `CAPTION` — photo-attached explanation;
- `MICRO` — folio, small editorial metadata, annotations.

### Direction

- prefer approximately 2–3 coherent type-family systems rather than many unrelated fonts;
- create variety through weight, scale, outline, hard shadow, vertical treatment, rotation and letter-spacing, not font proliferation;
- treat large Japanese type as a shape that may strongly collide with photography/art;
- inspect kinsoku, punctuation, kana/Latin balance, line breaks and optical alignment;
- avoid shrinking body text to rescue a crowded page;
- small white/light type on bright cyan/yellow/photography requires real contrast support.

Large display type should still read as a deliberate silhouette when the page is blurred or viewed at thumbnail scale.

---

## 5. Editorial copy voice

The copy should feel edited, specific and human rather than generic wedding-template text.

Prefer:
- short, energetic headlines;
- grounded phrases tied to a real episode/photo/detail;
- small moments of humor when true to the material;
- Japanese-first meaning;
- concise editorial labels.

Avoid:
- empty romantic clichés;
- generic AI/wedding phrases such as “最高の思い出がいっぱい”;
- invented editor quotes;
- meaningless decorative English;
- internal-schema language printed as reader-facing copy;
- fake dates/details used only to make a layout look full.

Decorative English may be used only when it has a real editorial job.

---

## 6. Publication DNA / unity

V20 should not look like eight unrelated posters, but it must also avoid one repeated template.

The target is approximately:
- majority page-specific personality;
- minority shared publication DNA.

Potential shared DNA candidates:
- folio/page-number behavior;
- one route-line drawing character;
- one small editorial-note tone;
- one keyline / hard-print outline behavior;
- one small stamp/meta grammar;
- one caption punctuation/marker behavior.

Do not freeze the final shared set before real pages prove which devices deserve repetition.

Never repeat the exact same component position/size just to create consistency.

---

## 7. Page temperature / publication rhythm

V20 needs emotional tempo.

- P01 — highest first-impact / poster energy;
- P02 — lively, personal, quick discoveries;
- P03 — more readable and story-led;
- P04–P05 — visual and emotional peak;
- P06 — candid, playful, human;
- P07 — today’s wedding discoveries, highly scannable but not timetable-like;
- P08 — deliberate release / afterglow.

Fail if all pages become equally dense, equally colorful, equally decorative or equally quiet.

---

## 8. P06–P07 spread relationship

Treat P06–P07 as a deliberate late-book narrative pair.

### P06 — `OFF THE MAP`
Answer: `WHO ARE THEY WHEN THEY ARE NOT TRAVELLING?`

Strong content roles:
- candid/daily-life photos;
- dogs when real photos exist;
- meaningful food/activity details;
- funny/awkward/human moments;
- best-shot captions;
- optional real micro Q&A/facts.

### P07 — `TODAY'S TRAVEL GUIDE / LOOK AROUND THE WEDDING`
Answer: `WHAT CAN GUESTS NOTICE AROUND TODAY'S WEDDING?`

Strong content roles only when verified:
- destination/table-theme ideas visible in the room;
- décor/flower/travel motifs;
- cake or physical object details;
- 2–4 couple-selected details with real meaning;
- `LOOK AROUND` / `EDITOR'S PICK` discoveries;
- `TODAY'S DESTINATION = YOKOHAMA` style editorial landing when grounded.

The narrative should feel:

`TRAVEL MEMORIES`
→ `REAL LIFE`
→ `TODAY`

P07 must not revert to dress code, access or schedule-first utility.

---

## 9. P08 closing quality

P08 must not become a generic thank-you card or leftovers page.

Aim for:
- one quiet real photograph or small closing collage;
- short sincere Japanese thank-you;
- minimal intentional issue/postcard metadata;
- one restrained closing device;
- visual relationship to P01 without copying its composition.

A continuation/journey concept such as `TO BE CONTINUED...` or `NEXT DESTINATION` may be tested as a tone device, but the final wording must remain native, deliberate and non-fabricated.

The emotional target is release, not another peak.

---

## 10. Magazine microdetails

Microdetails are the last 10–15% of polish.

Useful examples:
- folio/page number;
- `EDITOR'S PICK`;
- `LOOK AROUND`;
- tiny photo credit/annotation grammar where appropriate;
- destination stamp shell;
- issue/date metadata;
- arrow / pointer;
- small caption marker;
- tiny editorial note.

Rules:
- each must have an editorial job;
- they do not exist to fill empty space;
- meaningless English, fake barcode data, fake flight numbers or fabricated utility data are rejected;
- microdetails are not added until title/photo/hierarchy/reading path pass.

---

## 11. Online / third-party asset art-direction filter

A good internet asset can still damage the publication if its art direction conflicts with V20.

Before adoption, verify:
1. source / license / usage rights;
2. `LINE` — stroke weight and drawing language;
3. `COLOR` — palette and saturation behavior;
4. `TEXTURE` — paper/print/vector/photo character;
5. `DEPTH` — outline/shadow/flatness behavior;
6. `ERA` — contemporary, retro-print, scrapbook, etc.;
7. actual resolution / print suitability;
8. whether it is replaceable or destructive in Figma.

If two or more visual axes materially conflict, do not place it raw.

Choose one:
- rework it where the license permits;
- replace it;
- generate a V20-native equivalent;
- reject it.

The key question is:

> Does this look art-directed for the same publication?

—not merely “is this a nice asset?”.

Record provenance for any third-party production asset.

---

## 12. Image-generation quality system

Image generation is a primary production tool for difficult page-specific visual identity, not a random asset fountain.

Before generating a serious asset define:
- target page/spread;
- editorial job;
- intended physical size;
- approximate aspect ratio;
- crop allowance;
- negative-space requirement;
- transparent/opaque background;
- z-order expectation;
- intended strong overlaps;
- palette/texture family;
- source resolution target;
- forbidden AI tells;
- which authoritative text must NOT be baked in.

Good generation targets:
- original masthead art;
- large tropical/flower clusters;
- paper/ticket/ribbon editorial vessels;
- route front/back composite assets;
- stamp shells;
- page-edge composites;
- complex decorative objects that would look primitive if rebuilt atom-by-atom in Figma.

Reject:
- fake text;
- repeated petals/objects;
- mushy alpha edges;
- generic symmetric wreath/frame;
- glossy AI 3D lettering;
- stock clip-art spacing;
- generated autobiographical people/dogs used as real memories.

For high-impact assets, prefer 2–3 materially different serious options, then compare them inside the real Figma composition before promotion.

---

## 13. Professional critique lenses

A serious candidate should survive these lenses:

### Art Director
- one clear idea?
- memorable silhouette?
- visual personality rather than template styling?

### Editorial Designer
- correct reading order?
- intentional density?
- page job clear without explanation?

### Book Designer
- improves spread/page-turn rhythm?
- does not duplicate adjacent-page energy?
- contributes to one coherent publication?

### Japanese Typographer
- intentional line breaks?
- readable body/captions at A5?
- punctuation/spacing/Latin mix controlled?

### Photo Editor
- correct source for role?
- crop/scale/frame serve the photograph?
- repeated photo treatments avoided?

### Print Designer
- trim/bleed/fold/safe areas respected?
- thin lines / low contrast / dark shadows survive print?
- raster resolution sufficient?
- CMYK conversion risks visible?

A visually attractive candidate that fails a critical lens is not promoted.

---

## 14. Current priority for quality gains

The recommended order is:

1. real-photo discovery / role selection;
2. P01 real decorated cover assembly as the first visible quality benchmark;
3. P04–P05 real decorated center-spread assembly;
4. photo color/cohesion trials once enough real sources are selected;
5. Japanese display typography + editorial copy inside actual layouts;
6. P06–P07 narrative pair refinement;
7. P08 closing tone;
8. shared publication DNA after multiple real pages exist;
9. microdetails last.

Do not spend production time writing additional abstract design rules unless a real page reveals a missing decision principle.
