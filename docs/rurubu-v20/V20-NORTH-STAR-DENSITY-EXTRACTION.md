# 1. OVERALL OBSERVATION

The reference does **not** feel exciting merely because it is bright.

Its energy comes from repeated coexistence of:
- one large headline/title event;
- large real/photo-like visual weight;
- several smaller satellite images;
- high-frequency small labels and travel artifacts;
- active page edges;
- paper/photo/stamp/route overlap;
- multiple visual scales inside one page;
- localized cream/white reading zones;
- different page archetypes rather than one repeated template.

The single biggest lesson for V20 is:

`THE PAGE IS RICH BEFORE THE READER READS THE BODY COPY.`

The eye sees a complete editorial world from title silhouette + photography + paper artifacts + small discoveries.

# 2. VISUAL DENSITY CALIBRATION FROM THE REFERENCE

Observed working ranges across the reference pages. These are intentionally approximate and should be treated as calibration bands, not mechanical quotas.

## Major title event

Typical page:
- title/header zone visually occupies roughly the top 12–22% of page height;
- cover title can occupy roughly 25–35% of perceived page weight;
- titles commonly use thick outline/keyline, offset/shadow, banner or torn-paper support;
- titles are rarely isolated plain text.

Consequence for V20:
- major inside-page titles should usually feel like prepared graphic objects rather than 1–2 native text lines floating at the top.

## Photography

Observed archetypes:
- cover: HERO + several small support photos;
- profile: 2 main portraits + multiple smaller image placeholders/details;
- memories: roughly 5–6 unequal snapshots;
- venue/guide: 1 large HERO + roughly 4–6 support/detail images;
- schedule/information: only a few photos because typography/icons carry more weight;
- message/closing: low photo count, large calm writing field.

General consequence:
- dense travel-feature pages often feel substantially more photographic than current V20 sparse pages;
- however high information pages can replace photos with map/route/label artifacts.

## Micro discoveries

Typical dense reference page visibly contains many of:
- flowers/leaves;
- small planes/cameras/luggage;
- arrows/doodles;
- tiny stamps;
- small labels;
- short ribbons;
- little route fragments;
- issue marks;
- small caption fields;
- mini photos;
- tiny icons.

Working visual count on dense pages is often roughly `8–18 small discoverable events`, counting both meaningful micro-copy supports and decorative editorial cues.

Important: these are not 18 equal stickers. Many are tiny or partly hidden.

## Edge activity

Most reference pages activate multiple edges:
- flowers/foliage crop at corners;
- photos or paper objects push close to trim;
- title/ornament reaches into the top edge;
- luggage/camera/flowers enter lower corners;
- border/edge cluster helps the page feel physically full.

Working direction:
- energetic pages often have `2–4 active edges/corners`;
- calm pages still usually have at least one intentional edge treatment.

## Calm fields

Even the densest reference pages have local readability fields:
- cream paper;
- pale blue answer fields;
- clean photo-caption spaces;
- large message writing area;
- map field with legible title/body zones.

The calm field is usually **inside** the decorative world, not detached from it.

## Visual layers

Typical dense reference page can be read as:
1. paper/sky/base;
2. broad decorative environment;
3. HERO/photo group;
4. photo frames/backing;
5. title art;
6. subtitle/ribbon;
7. body/article field;
8. stamps/labels/icons;
9. flowers/objects/edge accents;
10. tiny meta.

Not every page uses ten literal layers, but the important point is that the reference rarely looks like only `background + photo + text`.

# 3. REFERENCE ARCHETYPE A — COVER

Observed behaviors:
- enormous `WEDDING` display mass dominates the upper third;
- thick white and colored outlining makes title readable over a busy sky/photo world;
- vertical logo/issue element creates a second title axis;
- a circular issue/date-style badge adds a small counterweight;
- main couple image occupies the center/lower visual core;
- architecture/destination environment remains visible and important;
- multiple small photo snapshots sit along the bottom;
- left-side colored editorial hooks create a dense side column;
- flowers, plane, ribbons and small marks create edge-to-edge excitement;
- at least three scales are obvious: giant title, medium hero/hook/photo, tiny micro labels/objects.

What V20 should extract for P01:
- title must be a massive editorial object;
- cover can support more than one small hook without becoming a grid;
- support photos/details can create immediate browse depth;
- strong title/photo/environment collision is normal;
- multiple active edges make the cover feel like a publication, not a poster.

Do not copy:
- the exact commercial-like logo shape;
- exact hook labels/positions;
- exact flower distribution;
- exact bottom photo row.

---

# 4. REFERENCE ARCHETYPE B — WORLD / ROUTE JOURNEY PAGE

Observed behaviors:
- broad cyan/sky environment acts as an active background;
- route/world map is a large middle-layer visual, not a literal navigation UI;
- dashed journey path provides a reading connector;
- several snapshots cluster around the lower half;
- suitcase, camera, globe, plane and ticket elements are used at different scales;
- florals concentrate at lower corners/edges rather than evenly filling every gap;
- metadata/date/location block grounds the page at the bottom;
- the page is dense but still has large blue negative space.

What V20 should extract for P04–P05/P07:
- a route can carry the eye without becoming a functional map;
- large background travel art may remain mostly behind photos/text;
- multiple snapshots can be concentrated in one region while another region stays open;
- travel objects work best as clusters, not isolated icon stickers.

---

# 5. REFERENCE ARCHETYPE C — PROFILE PAGE

Observed behaviors:
- title uses large Japanese display with small English subtitle on a paper/banner structure;
- two portraits are framed like printed snapshots rather than generic UI cards;
- profiles contain many short factual lines rather than one long paragraph;
- the two people receive parallel editorial importance but not a completely empty/minimal treatment;
- a story/timeline strip occupies a separate right-side system;
- bottom area contains several smaller image slots and a separate “favorite”/shared-life feature;
- dog, luggage, flowers, camera and small stamps fill corners and transitions;
- content is structured but still visually tactile.

Useful lesson for V20 P02:
- profile richness comes from many **short editorial fragments** attached to people, not from large biography paragraphs;
- 2–4 small fact clusters per person can be more magazine-like than one clean form;
- a separate shared-life mini-feature gives a second-look reward;
- generated profile vessels should vary rather than become repeated pills.

Caution:
- do not blindly reproduce the reference’s repeated fact-row layout; V20 should remain more asymmetric and less form-like.

---

# 6. REFERENCE ARCHETYPE D — MEMORIES / PHOTO FEATURE

Observed behaviors:
- large Japanese title + small English subtitle;
- short intro copy sits directly under title and is integrated with decorative doodle/plane/camera cues;
- roughly six photos of different sizes/orientations form the core story;
- photos are primarily white-paper snapshot frames but vary in rotation/scale;
- a large lower photo creates a secondary visual anchor;
- little hearts, route/doodle marks and flowers connect empty pockets;
- luggage/camera/flowers create a strong lower edge ending;
- there is very little truly “unused” space even where the background remains cream.

Useful lesson for V20 P03/P04/P06:
- a photo-led memory page often needs more satellites than a clean web/gallery layout;
- “calm” does not require empty lower halves;
- small notes and doodles can connect photographs without becoming a grid;
- photo size inequality is essential.

Caution:
- V20 should not frame every photo identically or tilt everything equally.

---

# 7. REFERENCE ARCHETYPE E — VENUE / GUIDE PAGE

Observed behaviors:
- large title with bilingual support at top;
- one large venue HERO occupies major left-side weight;
- four or more smaller support photos build context on the right;
- short body copy is placed below/near the HERO rather than isolated in a separate card;
- a large illustrated/map field fills much of the lower region;
- one small inset image interrupts the map;
- floral corner/edge clusters make the map and photography feel like one printed feature.

Useful lesson for V20 P07:
- a guide page can mix `one main visual + several small recognisable details + map/route-like environment + short explanatory notes`;
- informational art and photography can overlap rather than occupying separate modules;
- page edges remain active even on a more practical/information-heavy page.

Content warning:
- the reference venue-guide content itself is not V20 P07 authority. V20 remains seated-guest discovery, not access/navigation.

---

# 8. REFERENCE ARCHETYPE F — SCHEDULE / ORGANIZED INFORMATION PAGE

Observed behaviors:
- very strong title banner;
- vertical chronology is made lively with icons, differently colored time labels and photography;
- a separate side field holds dress-code/style information;
- three or more content types coexist without one repeated card component;
- floral/title border keeps a practical page inside the same publication world.

Useful lesson only:
- dense structured information can remain lively when iconography, photo inserts, labels and side content use unequal scales.

Do **not** transfer schedule content into V20 P07.

---

# 9. REFERENCE ARCHETYPE G — Q&A PAGE / WARNING CASE

Observed behaviors:
- strong large title;
- six repeated question/answer fields;
- surrounding camera, food, flowers, house, dog and travel cues maintain decorative energy.

Useful lesson:
- a repeated information system can still feel printed when title/edge ornaments are strong.

Important anti-pattern for V20:
- this is also the clearest reference example of why V20 must avoid six identical rounded modules;
- use the title/edge energy, not the repeated-card structure.

---

# 10. REFERENCE ARCHETYPE H — MESSAGE / CLOSING PAGE

Observed behaviors:
- title and subtitle are still visually authored even though page density is reduced;
- a huge calm cream writing field dominates;
- stamps/flowers occupy corners and edges;
- lower area contains one stronger callout with camera/travel objects;
- the closing page remains decorated, but its central reading field is much calmer than the cover/memory pages.

Useful lesson for V20 P08:
- “quiet ending” does not mean undecorated;
- one or two deliberate edge/callout events can maintain publication DNA while preserving a large calm field/photo.

# 13. WHY CURRENT V20 CAN STILL FEEL ~40% OF TARGET ENERGY

Even with strong page architecture, the current booklet can still feel materially calmer than the North-Star when:
- a page has only 1–3 medium objects and almost no micro discoveries;
- large cream areas lack a deliberate editorial function;
- titles are native text without a strong material silhouette;
- photos are framed/placed too politely;
- prepared parts remain isolated rather than interacting across layers;
- page edges are inactive;
- the same simple Figma primitive grammar remains visible;
- weak old SVG-derived parts survive as the vocabulary between photos/text;
- the page lacks the “second and third thing to discover” after the initial headline.

Therefore the next production phase should improve **density quality and depth**, not merely add more decoration.

# 15. FINAL PRINCIPLE

The North-Star is exciting because there is always another visual reason to keep looking, while the first-read event remains obvious.

V20 should therefore pursue:

`BIG PROMISE`
→ `PHOTO / FEATURE EVENT`
→ `SECONDARY STORY`
→ `SMALL DISCOVERY`
→ `ANOTHER SMALL DISCOVERY`
→ `CALM READING ISLAND`
→ `EDGE / PAGE-TURN CUE`

not:

`TITLE`
→ `PHOTO`
→ `BOX`
→ `EMPTY SPACE`.

The target is not random clutter. The target is **edited abundance**.
