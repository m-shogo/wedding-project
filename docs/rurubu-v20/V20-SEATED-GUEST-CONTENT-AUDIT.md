# V20 Seated-Guest Content Audit

Status: `CANONICAL_CONTENT_FILTER`

Primary use moment: guests are already inside the wedding venue and seated.

V20 is therefore a table-side editorial/profile/travel booklet. It is not an invitation, access guide, reception guide, seating chart or timetable handout.

## Editorial objective

Every interior module should do at least one of these:

1. help a guest know SHOGO/SHIORI better;
2. reveal a real story/memory;
3. create a conversation topic at the table;
4. make the physical wedding around the guest more fun to notice;
5. become a keepsake worth rereading after the wedding.

If a module does none of these, remove it.

## Explicitly remove / do not allocate page space

- dress code;
- access / station / parking;
- arrival/check-in instructions;
- RSVP;
- what to bring;
- generic etiquette;
- schedule-first/timetable page;
- ceremony start/end information as a major feature;
- seating-chart function;
- menu/drink duplication;
- escort-card/ticket function duplication;
- fake airline data / gate / boarding / flight numbers;
- generic wedding advice.

Known schedule facts may remain in project records, but they are not premium V20 editorial content for seated guests.

## Keep / strengthen

### P02 profile

Keep only facts that are personal enough to start conversation.

Good examples when factual:
- surprising hobby;
- favorite trip/place;
- favorite food/activity;
- personality contrast;
- dog/couple-life fact;
- a short answer that sounds like the person.

Weak profile filler to avoid:
- generic blood type/star sign fields unless personally meaningful;
- long resume/job descriptions;
- facts guests are unlikely to care about.

### P03 story

Prefer memorable episodes over dates.

Good:
- early outings;
- funny/recognizable relationship episode;
- how they became closer;
- proposal context;
- registration/wedding arrival.

Avoid:
- corporate timeline appearance;
- invented exact dates;
- every event treated equally.

### P04–P05 travel

Strong center-spread content because it matches both the couple and wedding theme.

Prioritize:
- real destinations;
- real photos;
- one memorable episode/detail per destination;
- Hawaii proposal as emotional peak;
- Yokohama as today's arrival point.

Avoid turning it into a generic tourist guide.

### P06 real life / best shots

Keep the human, slightly imperfect material.

Good:
- candid photos;
- dogs;
- food/activity with a real story;
- funny/ordinary moments;
- tiny captions that reveal personality.

Avoid only using polished travel portraits.

## P07 replacement: seated wedding discoveries

P07 should answer:

> `Now that you are here, what is fun to notice about today's wedding?`

Recommended content architecture:

### A. TODAY'S DESTINATION

Native anchor:
- `YOKOHAMA`
- `2026.10.24`
- short editorial line connecting the couple's journeys to today.

### B. LOOK AROUND — 3 to 6 discoveries

Only use verified physical details.

Candidate categories:
- destination-themed tables/decor;
- flowers/decor concept;
- travel objects/signage/paper motifs visible in the room;
- cake/design detail when final and visible;
- one hidden/easter-egg observation if genuinely present;
- one small couple-made/detail story.

Do not catalog everything. Curate the most interesting discoveries.

### C. EDITOR'S PICK — 2 to 4 details

These should be things the couple intentionally chose and can explain in one or two lines.

Examples of category, not facts:
- `WHY THIS THEME?`
- `OUR FAVORITE DETAIL`
- `LOOK FOR THIS`
- `MADE FOR TODAY`

Do not invent intent. Final copy waits for verified detail/meaning.

### D. Destination-theme visualization

If table/decor destinations are confirmed, display them as a playful travel-magazine cluster, not a seating map.

Good:
- unequal destination stamps;
- small regional silhouettes/illustrative cues;
- route fragments;
- clustered labels;
- one or two highlighted destinations with a real reason.

Bad:
- all destinations in identical chips/cards;
- table-number directory;
- tiny literal floor map without a guest need;
- suggesting where someone should sit.

## P07 content priority order

1. actual physical details guests can see now;
2. couple intention/story behind those details;
3. travel-theme discoveries;
4. decorative editorial micro-copy;
5. optional photo.

A photo is not required. P07 can be excellent with zero photos if the editorial vessels/illustrations are strong.

## Duplication rule

V20 should complement the other wedding paper items.

Do not duplicate functional content owned elsewhere simply because space exists.

The desired relationship is:
- other paper item = `WHAT DO I NEED TO KNOW/DO?`
- V20 = `WHO ARE THESE TWO / WHAT IS THEIR STORY / WHAT IS FUN TO NOTICE TODAY?`

## Copy gate

Before P07 final copy is locked, verify:
- final destination/table/decor concepts;
- which details will actually be present on the day;
- any intended meaning behind them;
- whether a cake/design feature is final enough to reveal;
- whether any surprise should remain a surprise.

Anything not verified stays semantic placeholder or is omitted.

## Success test

A seated guest should be able to flip to P07 and say something like:

- “Oh, that's why this is here.”
- “Our table is themed around this destination.”
- “I didn't notice that detail.”
- “This feels like them.”

If the likely reaction is only “I already knew the time/access/menu,” the content does not belong in P07.
