# NRSL — Travel energy must not erase artifact-role specificity

Source scope/item: `non-Rurubu / WEDDING PASSPORT`

State: `VERIFIED_LOCAL → CROSS_ITEM_CANDIDATE`

Date: 2026-08-22

## Visible problem

The selected WEDDING PASSPORT `FIELD JOURNAL / RETURN NOTE` remains structurally sound and recognizably passport/keepsake-like, but its deliberate restraint raised a valid art-direction question under the current travel / flight / Hawaii warmth / pop / excitement brief: could stronger visual energy improve pickup appeal without returning to fake-airline or generic tropical decoration?

## Root-cause hypothesis

A large factual/typographic gesture and bolder travel vernacular can increase anticipation more effectively than adding many small travel motifs. However, increasing energy can also flatten an item into a generic event/travel poster if the physical artifact role stops controlling the composition.

Therefore `emotional energy` and `artifact-role specificity` must be evaluated as separate axes.

## Bounded experiment

A fresh blank-frame Passport study was authored without duplicating or visually reusing current/legacy nodes:

- page `186:2 / REJECTED_STUDY / PASSPORT / DEPARTURE BROADSHEET / 2026-08-22`
- front `186:3 / REJECTED / PASSPORT FRONT / DEPARTURE BROADSHEET`
- back `186:18 / REJECTED / PASSPORT BACK / AFTERGLOW BROADSHEET`

Only verified non-visual requirements were re-entered: `1480×2100`, passport/keepsake role, `2026.10.24`, `YOKOHAMA`, couple-name role and issue-information role.

The tested treatment used large Japanese display type, an oversized native factual `10.24`, high-energy flat fields and one movement rule. It intentionally avoided fake flight numbers, gates, classes, barcodes, stamps, credentials and stock tropical imagery.

Professional hypotheses consumed without copying literal work:

- Pentagram / United Airlines — one genuine identity element can become larger and more expressive rather than accumulating small aviation clichés.
- Pentagram / Impala — everyday travel vernacular can communicate anticipation when it remains bold, colourful and ownable rather than admin/tech dominated.

## Expected improvement

Raise first-glance excitement and travel anticipation while preserving Japanese-first hierarchy and avoiding generic airline cosplay.

## Regression risk

A design can become more energetic and still become less appropriate. In particular, colour fields and oversized typography may turn a keepsake/booklet artifact into a generic event broadside.

## What must remain item-specific

Do not transfer:

- cobalt/coral/yellow/lagoon palette;
- vertical side signal;
- oversized `10.24` scale;
- broadside composition;
- specific Japanese copy;
- Passport physical grammar.

## Cross-item applicability hypothesis

When another item appears visually too quiet, do not score a bolder candidate only on excitement. Independently score:

1. emotional energy / pickup appeal;
2. semantic or physical artifact-role specificity;
3. information functionality;
4. suite-level distinctness.

A gain in the first axis does not compensate for losing the second.

## Next receiving-item experiment

Use this method on a materially different non-Rurubu artifact only when a screenshot-supported problem is genuinely `too quiet / low anticipation`. Create a bounded clean-room alternative and compare whether the increase in energy strengthens the item's real action/object identity. If it becomes a generic poster, ticket, card, envelope or event graphic unrelated to the specific role, reject rather than merge attractive fragments by default.
