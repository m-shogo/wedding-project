# Professional Design Council vNext — Wedding Travel / Flight / Hawaii / Pop Joy

Status: `PROJECT-WIDE_DESIGN_QUALITY_AUTHORITY / NEW-VNEXT-BAR`
Opened: 2026-08-20
Purpose: raise all wedding design work from competent/functional execution to veteran professional art-direction quality.

This document defines the design personas, critique process, learning sources, and visual-quality gate for the next full redesign cycle. It does not authorize cross-scope mutation by itself: Rurubu and non-Rurubu production firewalls remain intact. Each owner may consume this document as neutral project-wide design judgment and must still follow its own current authority.

## Why this reset exists

The existing suite accumulated strong structural QA, editability, rollback discipline, long-copy stress, clean-room rebuilds, and proof-language cleanup. Those are necessary but insufficient. The next problem is not implementation correctness; it is senior-level visual judgment.

The new brief is:

> Make every item feel like a joyful departure day: travel, flight, tropical sunlight, Hawaii warmth, anticipation, celebration, surprise, and movement — more pop, more fun, more memorable — while remaining credible professional print design rather than airport UI cosplay or AI-template decoration.

Prior `SELLABLE_VISUAL_QA_PASS` states become historical evidence, not a waiver from the vNext professional redesign cycle.

## Professional Design Council personas

Every materially new direction is critiqued through all of these roles. One AI does not pretend they are one interchangeable taste; each role asks different questions.

### 1. Executive Creative Director — 20+ year identity/editorial veteran

Profile:
- 20+ years across brand identity, editorial campaigns, hospitality, events, and print systems.
- Has led multi-touchpoint identity systems from concept through production.
- Rejects visual activity that has no concept.

Questions:
- What is the one sentence idea?
- Would this be recognizable with the logo removed?
- Is there one memorable gesture rather than ten small decorations?
- Does the suite have family resemblance without template sameness?
- Would I confidently present this as a paid agency proposal?

Vetoes:
- generic Canva/AI-template look;
- decorative airport vocabulary without semantic purpose;
- style assembled from isolated trends rather than one art direction.

### 2. Senior Japanese Editorial Designer — 15+ year print specialist

Profile:
- Japanese magazine, book, invitation, poster, and package experience.
- Strong in Japanese line breaking, optical alignment, vertical rhythm, display/body contrast, and print density.

Questions:
- Does Japanese type carry the composition, or is English decoration doing the work?
- Are line endings, measure, leading, punctuation, and optical spacing credible?
- Is whitespace intentional and active rather than simply empty?
- Does the page have editorial rhythm at thumbnail, reading, and actual size?

Vetoes:
- microcopy that only looks sophisticated on screen;
- centered-everything layouts;
- equal-card/grid repetition that reads as web UI.

### 3. Airline / Wayfinding Identity Designer — 15+ year transport specialist

Profile:
- airline identity, airport wayfinding, ticketing, signage, and environmental systems.
- understands what makes transport graphics feel authentic without inventing credentials.

Questions:
- Which cues are real paper/wayfinding functions and which are cosplay?
- Is movement created by scale, crop, direction, and rhythm rather than fake barcodes/stamps/checkpoints?
- Can key information be found in one glance?
- Does the artifact feel like travel while staying obviously wedding-specific?

Vetoes:
- fake flight number/class/gate/barcode/passport credential unless factually real and required;
- progress-stepper dots, scanner reticles, admin rails, or fake machine language used only to look “aviation”.

### 4. Destination / Hawaii Brand Art Director — culture-sensitive tropical specialist

Profile:
- destination branding, hospitality, premium leisure, tropical editorial campaigns.
- treats Hawaii as a real place and culture, not a bundle of clip-art stereotypes.

Questions:
- Does the design communicate warmth, welcome, sunlight, nature, hospitality, and a sense of place?
- Are the colors joyful and vivid without copying an existing airline identity?
- Are organic forms and imagery grounded in landscape, air, ocean, botanical growth, horizon, lei-like flow, light, or local materiality rather than caricature?
- Does “Hawaii” feel authentic and generous rather than kitsch?

Vetoes:
- random hibiscus/palm/ukulele/tiki accumulation;
- cultural symbols used without understanding;
- direct imitation of Hawaiian Airlines or Hawaii Tourism Authority protected identity assets.

### 5. Senior Wedding / Experiential Designer — emotional-journey specialist

Profile:
- wedding stationery, events, guest journey, spatial touchpoints, keepsake design.
- evaluates emotion before graphic cleverness.

Questions:
- What does the guest feel when this is handed to them?
- Is there anticipation, discovery, surprise, warmth, or delight?
- Is the object worth keeping after the event?
- Does each item have a clear role in the wedding journey rather than only looking consistent?

Vetoes:
- cold corporate systems;
- over-minimal “luxury” that removes joy;
- gimmicks that compete with the couple or ceremony.

### 6. Print Production Director — 20+ year prepress/physical-production specialist

Profile:
- offset/digital print, paper, finishing, trim, fold, perforation, punch, QR, legibility, color and proofing.

Questions:
- Does this work at actual physical size?
- Are type, rules, overprint-like textures, crop, safe area and finishing credible?
- Is an effect better achieved through paper/ink/finish rather than digital decoration?
- Can the final item be edited without rebuilding it?

Vetoes:
- screen-only microtype;
- invisible low-contrast helper copy;
- details that collapse after printing;
- unsafe fold/trim/punch interactions.

### 7. Image Editor / Art Buyer — visual-source specialist

Profile:
- campaign photography, editorial image selection, image-generation direction, licensing/provenance.

Questions:
- Does the image have a job: hero, atmosphere, place, texture, or narrative?
- Is the crop strong at the actual printed role?
- Would a real photo, generated non-person image, illustration, or no image be strongest?
- Does the image feel intentional rather than stock?

Vetoes:
- generated people presented as real guests/family;
- fake documentary wedding moments;
- low-resolution imagery enlarged to create impact;
- decorative image generation without a diagnosed need.

## External professional learning incorporated

### Pentagram — United Airlines

Transferable lesson: a transport brand can feel more energetic by making one genuine identity element bigger, bolder and more expressive, including allowing it to sweep beyond the frame. The lesson is not to copy United; it is to prefer one confident gesture over many small aviation clichés.

### Hawaiian Airlines / Lippincott identity refresh

Transferable lesson: premium leisure can be bright, emotive, warm and culturally grounded. Successful refresh work studied history, frontline experience and culture; it retained meaningful identity constants while making expression bolder. The visual system combines vivid color, organic natural forms, typography, and hospitality rather than relying on literal travel icons.

### Hawaii Tourism Authority / Hawaii brand guidance

Transferable lesson: depict Hawaii with authenticity, respect for place, natural environment, people, culture and hospitality. Do not reduce the destination to generic paradise clichés. Project work must not copy official logos or protected brand assets.

### Adobe graphic-design principles

Transferable lesson: hierarchy, emphasis, scale, contrast, repetition, rhythm, balance, alignment and negative space work as a connected system. “Pop” is not the number of decorations; it is controlled contrast, scale and rhythm that deliberately directs attention.

## Visual language

### Color behavior

Use high-energy color relationships, not one fixed palette copied across every artifact. The family may draw from:
- deep ocean navy / ink blue for structure;
- hibiscus-like magenta or warm pink for emotional energy;
- sunrise coral / orange for celebration and warmth;
- lagoon cyan / mint / turquoise for freshness and movement;
- mango / sunny yellow for small bursts of joy;
- sand / warm cream for paper grounding;
- white as active light, not empty filler.

Rule: each item gets 1 dominant, 1 supporting, 1 surprise accent. Do not use every color everywhere.

### Typography

- Japanese is the narrative voice and primary emotional carrier.
- Large Japanese display type may be cropped, staggered, rotated slightly, or overlap fixed art when legibility is preserved.
- English is used only when it contributes artifact identity, destination character, or rhythm; not as generic filler.
- Pair one expressive display face with a calm editorial/support face; avoid “all medium weight sans”.
- Test at physical size before accepting microcopy.

### Shape / movement

Prefer:
- sweeping crop-through-frame gestures;
- arcs suggesting horizon, flight path, wind or lei-like flow when they have compositional purpose;
- asymmetric blocks that feel like movement;
- perforation, ticket notches, fold, punch, stamp-like physicality only when the object legitimately has that role;
- large organic fields and photo crops rather than clusters of badges/icons.

Avoid:
- progress dots;
- scanner crosshairs;
- fake barcode fields;
- equal pills/cards;
- route nodes with no actual destination meaning;
- ornamental rules that bind nothing.

### Pop / fun test

A design is not “pop” merely because it is colorful. It must satisfy at least three:
- one unexpectedly large scale shift;
- one joyful color contrast;
- one movement gesture;
- one human/warm editorial phrase;
- one physical-paper surprise;
- one memorable crop/overlap;
- one moment of discovery on flip/open/tear/fold.

## New professional critique gate — 100 points

A new vNext candidate is not preferred merely because it is different.

Score each at actual screenshots:

- Concept clarity / ownability: 15
- Emotional excitement / “want to pick it up”: 15
- Typography / Japanese editorial craft: 15
- Composition / hierarchy / rhythm: 15
- Travel-flight-Hawaii integration without cliché: 10
- Item-specific functionality: 10
- Physical print credibility: 10
- Editability / realistic content resilience: 5
- Family fit without template sameness: 5

Promotion requirements:
- total >= 85/100;
- no category below 7/10 equivalent;
- Executive Creative Director, Japanese Editorial Designer, and Print Production Director have no veto;
- whole / reading / actual-size QA all PASS;
- realistic long-copy where relevant PASS;
- old design is opened only after the new clean-room candidate is sufficiently complete to prevent legacy lock-in.

## Required vNext workflow

1. Read facts, dimensions, physical role, unresolved inputs — not old visual layout.
2. Write one-sentence emotional brief.
3. Produce 3 materially different art-direction thumbnails from blank frames.
4. Council critique before detail polishing.
5. Pick 1 direction; build native text / vector / image roles with hybrid authoring.
6. Generate imagery only for a defined role and only if it improves the concept.
7. Whole-item 500px QA.
8. Reading-scale QA.
9. Actual-size / print-detail QA.
10. Realistic long-copy and name stress.
11. Score with the 100-point professional rubric.
12. Only then compare against retained current design.
13. Promote only when the new direction clearly wins; otherwise iterate another clean-room direction.

## Full-suite reset decision

By explicit user direction on 2026-08-20, all non-Rurubu items are reopened for a **new vNext professional redesign pass**, even if earlier clean-room versions already had Sellable Visual PASS. Prior designs remain valuable rollback/history but are no longer the aspiration ceiling.

The goal is not to “fix” every existing layout. The goal is to ask, for every item, what a veteran agency team would propose today if the project arrived as a fresh brief with travel / flight / Hawaii / pop / fun / excitement as the emotional direction.
