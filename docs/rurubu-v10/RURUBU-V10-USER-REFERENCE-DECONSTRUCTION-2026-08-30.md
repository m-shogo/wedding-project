# 1. Why this reference is closer to the target

The reference succeeds because it behaves like a Japanese travel-information magazine rather than a wedding brochure or a web page.

Its strongest characteristics are:

1. **Immediate genre recognition**
   - giant `WEDDING` display title;
   - small stacked `るるぶ`-like mark;
   - saturated cyan/pink/yellow travel-magazine palette;
   - many cover lines and small discoveries;
   - travel motifs and location photography.

2. **Dense but hierarchical pages**
   - pages contain many objects, but one or two elements remain dominant;
   - density is concentrated in clusters rather than distributed as equal cards;
   - calm paper/sky zones still exist for readable copy.

3. **Strong scale contrast**
   - giant title vs tiny captions;
   - hero photo vs small polaroids;
   - one large floral cluster vs scattered single flowers;
   - large page title vs micro English subtitle.

4. **Controlled overlap**
   - flowers overlap frames and page edges;
   - photo frames partially overlap each other;
   - titles sit close to, and sometimes over, visual content;
   - stickers/ribbons cross other objects instead of living inside isolated cards.

5. **Travel editorial vocabulary**
   - airplane;
   - world map;
   - camera;
   - suitcase;
   - passport;
   - ticket;
   - route line;
   - date stamp;
   - location label;
   - polaroids;
   - tropical flowers and leaves.

6. **Non-uniform photo treatment**
   - hero photography;
   - polaroid frames;
   - stacked prints;
   - cutout-style objects;
   - portrait frames;
   - small gallery tiles;
   - map/venue images treated differently from people/travel photography.

7. **Editorial microcopy**
   - small hand-written or label-like phrases;
   - page subtitles;
   - section ribbons;
   - arrows and stamps;
   - short invitations to browse.

The target is not `more decoration`. The target is this entire editorial system working together.

# 2. Visual fingerprint

## 2.1 Palette behavior

Observed dominant families:
- bright lagoon/sky cyan;
- strong wedding pink/magenta;
- warm cream paper;
- sunny yellow;
- foliage/tropical green;
- small dark navy/ink details;
- coral/red accents.

Behavior:
- saturated color is used at title/edge/accent scale;
- longer copy usually sits on cream/light stable fields;
- pages alternate between blue-photo-heavy and cream-paper-heavy atmospheres;
- yellow is a high-energy marker, not a body-copy field;
- pink is used to make emotional/title moments pop.

Do not imitate every exact hue mechanically. Preserve the color jobs.

---

## 2.2 Typography behavior

Observed hierarchy:

### Level A — Hero display
Examples in the reference:
- huge `WEDDING` lettering;
- thick round Japanese page titles;
- white outline + colored fill + shadow/edge treatment.

Job:
- identify page/feature instantly;
- carry magazine identity;
- remain legible at thumbnail scale.

### Level B — Section ribbon / brush title
Observed:
- pink brush/ribbon under a page title;
- yellow banner or blue strip behind small headings;
- small English subtitle underneath Japanese title.

Job:
- add hierarchy and editorial voice;
- break rigid alignment.

### Level C — Native readable copy
Observed:
- dark Japanese text on cream/white;
- dates and labels with stronger color/weight;
- compact information blocks.

Job:
- factual information and story reading.

### Level D — Microcopy
Observed:
- handwritten English/Japanese accents;
- photo captions;
- small labels;
- stamps and postcard-like notes.

Job:
- reward closer inspection.

Critical lesson:

Do not use one font treatment for all text. The reference feels magazine-like because display, section, body and microcopy speak with different voices.

# 3. Edge behavior

A key reason the reference feels richer than generic AI layouts is how it uses the page edges.

Observed:
- palm leaves and flowers enter from corners;
- decorations partially crop at trim;
- photos sit close to edges in selected zones;
- title elements approach the upper boundary;
- lower-corner suitcase/camera/passport clusters create weight;
- not every object is centered inside a comfortable margin.

Rule:

> Use safe areas for critical information, not for all decoration.

Decorative edge tension is desirable. Critical text/face placement still follows trim and safe-area rules.

# 4. Decoration is clustered, not evenly sprinkled

The reference uses floral/travel decoration in recognizable clusters.

Common cluster types:

### `TROPICAL_CORNER_CLUSTER`
- 2–5 flowers;
- 1–3 leaves;
- optionally one travel object;
- sits at a corner or frame junction.

### `TRAVEL_OBJECT_CLUSTER`
- suitcase + passport + ticket;
- camera + stamp;
- airplane + route;
- small objects overlap each other.

### `TITLE_SUPPORT_CLUSTER`
- flower(s) + ribbon + tiny icon around a title;
- reinforces title without becoming a separate card.

### `PHOTO_JUNCTION_CLUSTER`
- flowers/leaves placed where two photo frames overlap;
- visually hides/softens geometry and creates tactile collage behavior.

Avoid one icon in every gap. Prefer 3–5 purposeful clusters per high-density page and fewer on quieter pages.

# 5. Reference panel-by-panel observations

The composite contains eight page-like panels plus a parts strip. These are treated below as visual lessons, not literal P01–P08 mapping.

---

## Panel A — Cover

Observed behavior:
- very large pink/white/yellow `WEDDING` title takes the top-middle;
- small `るるぶ`-style identity mark occupies upper-left;
- circular/organic badge in upper-right;
- couple hero photo dominates the middle/lower page;
- venue/church appears behind or adjacent to couple;
- left edge contains stacked cover lines with different colored treatments;
- bottom contains multiple small polaroid/travel images;
- tropical floral elements create edge/corner clusters;
- pink banner at lower-right invites the reader inward.

What to preserve:
- title silhouette must be unmistakable;
- hero couple image and title should overlap in visual territory;
- cover lines should feel editorial, not navigation buttons;
- bottom supporting photos must vary in width/angle/position;
- one page should contain multiple entry points while still having a clear first read.

Do not reproduce:
- literal fake `るるぶ` branding if rights/brand usage is not intended;
- generated incorrect names/text;
- exact copyrighted page composition.

---

## Panel B — World map / journey page

Observed behavior:
- full cyan sky/ocean visual field;
- handwritten-style `Wedding Journey` title near top;
- yellow world map creates central structure;
- airplane + dotted route animate the map;
- small polaroid/photo clusters appear mainly around edges/lower half;
- suitcase, sunglasses, drink, flowers create a travel-object still life;
- barcode/date/venue utility block sits at lower-left/center.

Lesson:
- large quiet graphic field can coexist with dense lower clusters;
- route/map gives semantic motion;
- utility information can be visually separate without becoming a web card.

---

## Panel C — Profile + story hybrid

Observed behavior:
- large pink page title on cream field;
- two primary portrait photos in polaroid-style frames;
- names appear directly associated with portraits;
- factual profile fields are compact and aligned but not wrapped in giant cards;
- right side has a vertical mini-history/timeline;
- lower section introduces a `Best 5` style strip with small photo slots;
- corgi and floral/travel decorations sit at lower-right.

Lesson:
- structured information can remain magazine-like when hierarchy is driven by typography and small labels rather than rounded cards;
- two people can be balanced without perfect symmetry;
- a small secondary editorial feature at the bottom increases browse value.

---

## Panel D — Memory journey page

Observed behavior:
- page title at top with English subtitle;
- short intro copy sits in a calm cream area;
- many travel photos occupy most of the page;
- frames vary slightly in size and angle;
- one lower-left/lower-middle image is noticeably larger;
- travel doodles, airplane, camera, heart and flowers surround images;
- suitcase anchors lower-right.

Lesson:
- photography should carry the page;
- decorative objects should frame or connect photography, not compete equally;
- one photo must dominate the gallery.

---

## Panel E — Venue guide

Observed behavior:
- title has strong blue outline and floral support;
- venue name and short copy appear at upper-left;
- one large venue image anchors the left/middle;
- multiple smaller venue/detail photos form an irregular right-side cluster;
- description copy sits below hero image;
- map occupies a clear bottom band;
- flowers and small illustrations mark corners and transitions.

Lesson:
- useful guide information can be dense without becoming a dashboard;
- map is a genuine semantic region;
- hero + satellite images create strong magazine hierarchy.

---

## Panel F — 1DAY schedule

Observed behavior:
- top title is large and energetic;
- left/middle contains a chronological vertical sequence with colored time labels;
- each stop uses a small icon;
- right column contains dress-code/fashion illustration, small color chips and supporting visuals;
- lower-right adds a `Special Thanks` editorial note;
- flowers and travel/celebration motifs frame the page.

Lesson:
- repeated time labels are acceptable because chronology genuinely requires repetition;
- the repeated structure is interrupted by a second editorial cluster;
- utility page still needs a visual reward, not only rows.

---

## Panel G — Q&A

Observed behavior:
- six question areas exist, but they are not generic software cards;
- Q-number labels are irregular colored bursts/labels;
- white answer spaces have varied supporting illustrations and objects;
- questions are anchored with color + number + nearby motif;
- lower-right corgi and floral edge cluster adds personality;
- strong title remains separate from answer system.

Lesson:
- repeated semantic items can share a system without looking like a UI component grid;
- vary edge details, icons and visual entry points while keeping answer text readable;
- keep authoritative answers native/editable.

---

## Panel H — Guest message / back-page behavior

Observed behavior:
- large calm lined writing/message field dominates the upper two-thirds;
- postcard stamp/postmark creates thematic utility;
- lower area contains photo contest CTA, camera, hashtag-like labels, passport/ticket/suitcase cluster;
- flowers/foliage frame edges heavily while central message field stays calm.

Lesson:
- a quiet page can still share the publication identity through its edges;
- density can live at the perimeter while the functional writing/reading zone remains open.

This reference should influence:
- density;

- title scale;
- asymmetry;
- cluster behavior;
- photo variety;
- travel-editorial vocabulary;
- edge tension;
- color jobs;

# 8. Reference-distance questions for every page

When comparing a V10 page to this approved direction, ask:

1. Is the page genre recognizable in 3 seconds?
2. Is there at least one large scale contrast?
3. Does one photo/visual clearly dominate where the page is photo-led?
4. Are supporting photos different enough in aspect/scale/treatment?
5. Is there a calm field for body copy?
6. Do decorative elements form purposeful clusters rather than random sprinkles?
7. Do at least some elements push toward edges/overlap?
8. Does the page contain micro-discoveries without losing first-read clarity?
9. Does the page look like editorial print rather than software UI?
10. Can photos/text still be replaced in Figma?

If the answer to 2, 3, 4, 6 or 9 is no, fix structure before adding more decoration.
