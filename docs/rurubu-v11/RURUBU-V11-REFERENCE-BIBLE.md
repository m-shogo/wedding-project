# 1. The reference is successful because it behaves like print editorial, not UI

Core traits:
- one unmistakable dominant entry point per page;
- large scale contrast between hero and support elements;
- multiple photo treatments on the same page;
- asymmetrical cluster composition;
- selective overlap and slight rotation;
- high density concentrated in zones;
- calm cream/light reading fields inside dense pages;
- edge-cropped foliage/objects;
- saturated travel colors with clear jobs;
- many small discoveries without destroying scan order;
- visible tactile-object language: ticket, passport, camera, suitcase, stamp, polaroid, route, flowers.

The correct instruction is never merely `add more stickers`.

---

# 2. Five-level hierarchy

Every V11 page should define:

1. `FIRST_READ` — hero title or dominant visual.
2. `SECOND_READ` — main photo/story cluster.
3. `EDITORIAL_SUPPORT` — section labels/supporting imagery.
4. `UTILITY` — facts/dates/times/locations/captions.
5. `MICRO_DISCOVERY` — small stamps, arrows, notes, icons, jokes.

All five levels must not have equal visual weight.

---

# 3. Photo grammar

Target behavior:
- one hero or a clearly intentional dual-hero;
- support photos differ visibly in area;
- mix landscape/portrait/square/polaroid/cutout/organic crops;
- two or more supports may overlap;
- 1–3 support frames may rotate slightly;
- a photo can approach trim/edge where safe;
- frames remain separate from photo sources;
- all provisional photos remain replaceable.

Reject:
- 3–6 equal cards;
- identical aspect ratio across a whole page without semantic reason;
- identical border/radius everywhere;
- gallery as a perfect grid by default.

---

# 4. Title grammar

Strong V11 title behavior can include:
- large rounded display lettering;
- white keyline/outline;
- offset shadow;
- hot-pink / blue / yellow contrast;
- brush/ribbon plate;
- small English subtitle;
- flowers/leaves partially crossing title zone;
- title positioned close to visual content rather than isolated in a clean web header.

Do not rasterize long/changeable body copy into title art.

---

# 5. Color jobs

Reference color families:
- bright sky/lagoon cyan;
- hot pink/magenta;
- sunny yellow;
- tropical green;
- warm cream/paper;
- dark navy/ink;
- coral/red micro-accent.

Use colors by job:
- cyan = travel/sky/freshness/large energetic field;
- pink = emotional/title emphasis;
- yellow = burst/highlight;
- green = foliage/travel support;
- cream = readable editorial field;
- dark ink = factual/body structure.

Do not make every page use every color at equal saturation.

---

# 6. Decoration grammar

Decoration should appear as authored clusters.

Preferred cluster families:
- `TROPICAL_CORNER`: flowers + leaves entering from edge;
- `PHOTO_JUNCTION`: flower/leaf sitting where photo frames meet;
- `TRAVEL_STILL_LIFE`: suitcase + passport + ticket or camera + stamp;
- `TITLE_SUPPORT`: flower/ribbon/icon supporting a title;
- `ROUTE_CLUSTER`: airplane + dotted route + map/pin;
- `POSTCARD_CLUSTER`: postmark/stamp/ticket/message-line art.

Reject evenly sprinkling unrelated icons into every empty gap.

---

# 7. Edge tension

At least selected pages should use decorative edge pressure:
- foliage can crop into bleed;
- luggage/camera can anchor lower corners;
- a ribbon can approach an outer edge;
- one support photo may sit close to trim;
- title support art may enter the edge zone.

Critical text and important faces still remain safe.

---

# 8. Density behavior

Density is clustered, not uniform.

High-density pages usually have:
- one hero/title region;
- one photo-heavy cluster;
- one utility/info region;
- 2–5 purposeful micro-discoveries;
- one calm reading area.

A page can be busy while still having hierarchy.

---

# 9. Anti-AI / anti-Web tells

Immediate structural rejection if the result shows:
- dashboard/card grammar;
- equal rounded modules;
- all content centered;
- repeated Auto Layout columns controlling the whole page;
- uniform corner radius;
- generic soft gradient + shadow polish;
- giant luxury whitespace;
- decorative English with no editorial job;
- tiny factual text used to make room for decoration;
- all pages sharing the same title/photo composition.
